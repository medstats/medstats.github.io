  2. CSF CMV PCR Positivity Can Be a Bystander Distractor: Low-level CSF CMV PCR positivity in severe AIDS can represent non-pathogenic compartmental reactivation rather than true invasive CMV encephalitis, highlighting the risk of diagnostic anchoring.
  3. Clinical Relapse After Initial Response Signals Coverage Gaps: Transient clinical improvement on empiric broad-spectrum therapy followed by early relapse indicates unaddressed atypical pathogens (e.g., Nocardia requiring high-dose Bactrim/Imipenem/Linezolid).
  4. Negative Modified Acid-Fast Staining Does Not Rule Out Nocardia: Antimicrobial pre-treatment alters bacterial cell wall lipids, rendering modified Kinyoun AFB stains negative while Gram stain and GMS silver stain reveal thin branching filaments.
  5. Stereotactic Brain Biopsy Trumps Non-Invasive Diagnostics: In localized intracranial ring-enhancing lesions, non-invasive CSF studies may remain non-diagnostic; early stereotactic tissue biopsy is decisive.
  6. Intraocular Nocardiosis vs. CMV Retinitis Differentiation: Ocular involvement requires expert fundoscopic evaluation and ocular-fluid PCR to avoid unnecessary toxicity from prolonged anti-CMV therapy (e.g., Foscarnet/Ganciclovir).
  7. Scrotal and Epididymal Nocardiosis as a Diagnostic Portal: Genitourinary Nocardia abscesses provide an accessible peripheral tissue target for aspiration and 16S rRNA gene sequencing.
  8. Adrenal Axis Surveillance in Disseminated Nocardiosis: Bilateral adrenal lesions require serial monitoring for acute adrenal insufficiency and stress-dose steroid coverage during acute illness.
  9. Initial Multi-Drug Induction Regimens for CNS Nocardiosis: Empirical CNS Nocardia coverage should combine high-dose IV Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole with Imipenem/Meropenem or Linezolid pending species-level AST.
  10. Speciation via 16S Sequencing and Susceptibility Testing: Nocardia species have distinct resistance patterns (e.g., N. farcinica cephalosporin resistance); species identification via 16S sequencing is critical for tailoring long-term therapy.
  11. Therapeutic Dosing vs. Prophylactic Dosing: Maintenance oral Bactrim for CNS/disseminated nocardiosis requires weight-adjusted therapeutic dosing rather than standard single-tablet daily prophylaxis.
  12. Minimum 12-Month Duration of Therapy: Complete eradication of disseminated nocardiosis in immunocompromised hosts mandates at least 12 months of targeted antimicrobial therapy with serial imaging monitoring.
  13. ART Re-initiation Timing and IRIS Risk: Antiretroviral therapy (TLD) should be restarted within 1–2 weeks once acute neurological stability is established under antimicrobial coverage, with corticosteroids reserved for severe edema.
  14. Monitoring Drug Toxicities During Prolonged Bactrim Use: Long-term high-dose Bactrim demands regular surveillance of renal function, electrolytes (hyperkalemia), bone marrow counts, and liver enzymes.
  15. Criteria for Discontinuing Secondary Suppressive Therapy: Secondary prophylaxis for Nocardia should be maintained until the patient demonstrates sustained immune reconstitution (CD4 >200 cells/µL for >6 months on ART).
