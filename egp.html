Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Definition of EGPA & HES Overlap: Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA / Churg-Strauss Syndrome) and Hypereosinophilic Syndrome (HES) share a disease spectrum characterized by tissue-invasive hypereosinophilia, pulmonary infiltrates, and systemic multi-organ dysfunction.
  2. ANCA Negativity Frequency: Up to 50–60% of patients with EGPA are ANCA-negative; ANCA-negative disease tends to have less glomerulonephritis but a higher incidence of eosinophilic myocarditis and pericardial serositis.
  3. Diagnostic Threshold for Hypereosinophilia: An Absolute Eosinophil Count (AEC) exceeding 1,500 cells/µL defines severe peripheral hypereosinophilia; values above 3,000 cells/µL carry high risk for eosinophil-mediated organ toxicity.
  4. BAL Eosinophilia Benchmark: A Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL) differential containing >25% eosinophils (30% in this case) confirms severe eosinophilic alveolitis/bronchitis, securing diagnostic criteria even in the absence of necrotizing vasculitis on biopsy.
  5. Chest CT Centrilobular Nodularity: HRCT chest findings in pulmonary eosinophilia characteristically show centrilobular micro-nodules, tree-in-bud opacities, bronchial wall thickening, and hyper-expansion without dense lobar consolidation.
  6. Upper Airway & Sinus Involvement: Over 80% of EGPA patients suffer from chronic rhinosinusitis, nasal polyposis, or paranasal sinus air-fluid levels, serving as an important diagnostic hallmark.
  7. Eosinophilic Myocarditis Risk: Cardiac disease accounts for up to 50% of deaths in EGPA/HES; all patients with newly identified severe hypereosinophilia require baseline ECG, cardiac troponin, and echocardiography (or Cardiac MRI).
  8. Drug-Unmasked Vasculitis Phenomenon: Exposure to certain drugs (e.g., Levetiracetam, Dimethyl Fumarate, or leukotriene receptor antagonists) can trigger or unmask an underlying latent eosinophilic vasculitic diathesis.
  9. Targeted Anti-IL5 Biologic (Mepolizumab): Mepolizumab (Nucala 300mg SC monthly), a monoclonal antibody targeting Interleukin-5 (IL-5), inhibits eosinophil maturation and recruitment, offering a proven steroid-sparing therapy in EGPA.
  10. High-Dose Corticosteroid Induction: Systemic glucocorticoids (IV Methylprednisolone pulse followed by oral Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day) remain the first-line remission induction therapy for active EGPA/HES.
  11. Differentiation from DRESS/DIHS: While DRESS presents with drug rash and eosinophilia, chronic sinusitis, BAL eosinophilia >25%, pericardial effusion, and long-term relapse favor systemic eosinophilic vasculitis over self-limited drug eruption.
  12. Exclusion of Neoplastic HES: Patients with steroid-refractory hypereosinophilia require testing for the FIP1L1-PDGFRA fusion gene, JAK2 mutations, and T-cell receptor (TCR) gene rearrangements to exclude clonal eosinophilic leukemia.
  13. Infection Prophylaxis during Immunosuppression: Patients receiving high-dose steroids and biologics require Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) prophylaxis (TMP-SMX) and pre-treatment screening for Strongyloides stercoralis.
  14. Monitoring Disease Activity (BVAS Score): Serial monitoring of the Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score (BVAS), AEC, and serum IgE guides steroid tapering and evaluates response to maintenance biologics.
  15. Multidisciplinary Co-Management: Optimal long-term care requires close collaboration between Pulmonology, Rheumatology, Cardiology, and Allergy/Immunology to prevent disease relapses and irreversible organ damage.
