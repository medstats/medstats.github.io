Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Definition of IgG4-Related Hypophysitis: IgG4-Related Hypophysitis is a rare manifestation of systemic IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) characterized by fibroinflammatory infiltration of the pituitary gland, infundibular stalk, and adjacent skull base/sinuses.
  2. Central Diabetes Insipidus as a Key Pivot: Pituitary adenomas almost never cause diabetes insipidus; the triad of Central Diabetes Insipidus (CDI), infundibular stalk thickening, and sellar mass mandates evaluation for infiltrative, inflammatory, or granulomatous disorders.
  3. Seronegative IgG4 Spectrum: Normal serum IgG4 concentrations do NOT exclude IgG4-related disease; up to 30–40% of histopathologically proven IgG4-RD cases are seronegative prior to treatment.
  4. Pitfall of Bystander Low-Titer ANCA: Weakly positive MPO-ANCA (low titer) can occur as a non-specific bystander finding in IgG4-RD and should not lead to a misdiagnosis of Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA) without necrotizing vasculitis or granulomas on tissue.
  5. Cranial Neuropathy & Sinus Extension: Infiltrative IgG4-RD can extend from the sphenoid sinus to the cavernous sinus, causing cranial nerve palsies (such as CN VI sixth-nerve palsy and diplopia) and headache.
  6. Imaging Signature of Storiform Fibrosis: High-resolution T2-weighted MRI demonstrating hypointense, enhancing sinonasal mucosal thickening with sphenoid bone sclerosis reflects dense collagenous storiform fibrosis.
  7. Primacy of Pre-Treatment Tissue Biopsy: Transsphenoidal biopsy of pituitary or sinus tissue before initiating long-term immunosuppression is critical to exclude mimics such as invasive fungal sinusitis (Aspergillus), GPA, sarcoidosis, or lymphoma.
  8. Pathognomonic Histopathologic Triad: The classic histopathologic features of IgG4-RD include a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, storiform (woven-mat) fibrosis, and obliterative phlebitis without necrosis or true granulomas.
  9. Immunohistochemical Diagnostic Threshold: Demonstration of >10 to 14 IgG4-positive plasma cells per high-power field (HPF) and an elevated IgG4-to-IgG ratio on tissue section confirms the diagnosis of IgG4-RD.
  10. Reversibility of Diabetes Insipidus: Unlike long-standing ischemic or fibrotic stalk destruction, early treatment of IgG4-related infundibulohypophysitis can achieve complete functional reversal of Central Diabetes Insipidus and permit total desmopressin weaning.
  11. Role of Targeted B-Cell Depletion (Rituximab): Rituximab (1 g IV on Days 1 and 15) serves as a highly effective disease-modifying therapy by depleting short-lived CD20+ B-cell precursor pools that replenish fibrogenic IgG4+ plasmablasts.
  12. Glucocorticoid Induction & Taper: Initial therapy with high-dose oral Prednisone (60 mg daily with a 6-month structured taper) provides rapid anti-inflammatory debulking, while Rituximab anchors long-term remission.
  13. Systemic Staging Requirement: Whole-body imaging (FDG-PET/CT or CT chest/abdomen/pelvis) is recommended at diagnosis to screen for subclinical multi-organ IgG4-RD involvement (pancreas, retroperitoneum, thyroid, or salivary glands).
  14. Monitoring Pituitary Function: Comprehensive baseline and serial endocrine testing (cortisol, thyroid panel, testosterone/gonadotropins, IGF-1) is mandatory to detect anterior hypopituitarism and guide hormone replacement.
  15. Long-Term Relapse Surveillance: Because IgG4-RD carries a ~30% risk of late relapse following B-cell depletion, patients require serial clinical evaluations, endocrine profiling, and symptom-triggered neuroimaging.
