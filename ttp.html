Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Pathognomonic MAHA Smear Findings: Identifying schistocytes (≥2 per high-power field), polychromasia, and nucleated red blood cells on peripheral smear mechanically confirms Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA) and mandates immediate evaluation for thrombotic microangiopathies.
  2. The Platelet-to-Creatinine Discordance: Severe, profound consumption thrombocytopenia (<30,000/µL) accompanied by relatively preserved renal function (Creatinine <2.0 mg/dL) strongly favors Immune TTP over renal-dominant HUS or aHUS.
  3. PLASMIC Score as a Life-Saving Treatment Trigger: A PLASMIC score ≥6 predicts severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (<10%) with >90% specificity and mandates immediate initiation of Therapeutic Plasma Exchange (TPE) without waiting for confirmatory ADAMTS13 lab results.
  4. Three-Phase iTTP Treatment Strategy: Modern iTTP therapy comprises three distinct pillars: Rescue (Therapeutic Plasma Exchange), Shield (Caplacizumab anti-vWF nanobody), and Cure (Rituximab B-cell depletion).
  5. Mechanism of Caplacizumab (Anti-vWF Nanobody): Caplacizumab binds the A1 domain of von Willebrand factor multimers, blocking vWF-platelet interaction and microvascular thrombosis while plasma exchange and immunosuppression clear autoantibodies.
  6. Role of Early Rituximab B-Cell Depletion: High ADAMTS13 inhibitor autoantibody titers (>8.0 Bethesda Units) predict prolonged TPE dependence; initiating Rituximab early clears inhibitor-producing B-cell clones and lowers relapse rates.
  7. Avoidance of Platelet Transfusions in TTP: Platelet transfusions are contraindicated in TTP unless life-threatening hemorrhage occurs, as exogenous platelets fuel systemic microthrombus formation and worsen end-organ ischemia.
  8. Cardiac Microvascular Injury in TTP: Elevated cardiac troponin and ECG conduction abnormalities signal myocardial microthrombi and predict higher mortality, requiring continuous telemetry and blood pressure optimization.
  9. Distinguishing iTTP from STEC-HUS: While a diarrheal prodrome can trigger iTTP flares, negative Shiga-toxin testing, absence of severe acute kidney injury, and profound thrombocytopenia confirm iTTP over STEC-HUS.
  10. Negative Coombs Test Requirement: A negative Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT/Coombs) is essential to exclude autoimmune hemolytic anemia (Evans syndrome) before establishing a diagnosis of non-immune MAHA.
  11. Refractory TTP Resolution with Nanobody Therapy: In patients stalling on TPE and steroids (Day 5–10), initiating Caplacizumab rapidly restores platelet counts within 48 hours by neutralizing persistent ultra-large vWF multimers.
  12. Therapeutic Plasma Exchange Discontinuation Criteria: Daily TPE should be continued until platelet counts exceed 150,000/µL for at least 2 consecutive days alongside LDH normalization.
  13. Serial ADAMTS13 Surveillance: Monitoring ADAMTS13 activity every 3 months for 1–2 years enables detection of subclinical serologic drops (<20%) weeks before overt clinical thrombocytopenia recurs.
  14. Pre-emptive B-Cell Immunosuppression: Administering pre-emptive Rituximab when ADAMTS13 activity drops <20% during remission prevents overt clinical TTP relapse and avoids emergency plasma exchange.
  15. Patient Education & Trigger Avoidance: Educating patients on early relapse symptoms (dark urine, petechiae, focal weakness) and avoiding unmonitored surgical or infectious triggers is essential for long-term survival.
