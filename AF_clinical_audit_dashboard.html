Atrial Fibrillation Clinical Audit

Retrospective audit of 55 AF admissions from 2615 General Medicine discharge summaries. AF was identified from the discharge DIAGNOSIS and then expanded by searching COURSE DURING HOSPITALISATION with negation review.

The audit focuses on stroke-risk documentation, discharge anticoagulation, apixaban dosing, renal information, bleeding/hemorrhagic reasons for deferral, and rheumatic mitral stenosis / prosthetic-valve exceptions.

Headline finding

Anticoagulation appears under-used at discharge.
Among 40 routine discharges with documented minimum CHA₂DS₂-VA ≥2, only 18 (45.0%) had an OAC documented.
  • 22 eligible routine discharges had no OAC documented.
  • 6 of these had a major or possible bleeding/hemorrhagic concern documented.
  • 16 remain possible treatment gaps without an obvious bleeding explanation in the available summary.
  • These are audit flags, not definitive prescribing errors; contraindications and plans may exist outside the discharge summary.
  • Only 14 out of 55 (25.5 %) AF cases saw use of parenteral Anticoagulation, 8 LMWH ,5 UFH and 1 Fondaparinaux

Apixaban dosing sensitivity

Weight is not reliably available. For estimation only, this audit assumes weight ≤60 kg. Under the apixaban AF label, dose reduction to 2.5 mg BD requires at least two of: age ≥80, weight ≤60 kg, serum creatinine ≥1.5 mg/dL.

Learning points / messages

Case finding

Search beyond the diagnosis line

Searching Hospital Course added 4 AF admissions. Use explicit AF terms plus negation review to avoid false positives such as “no AF”.

Stroke prevention

Risk should be assessed in every AF admission

51/55 had documented minimum CHA₂DS₂-VA ≥2. A missing comorbidity in the summary may make the true score higher, not lower.

Discharge safety

“No OAC” needs a documented reason

Where OAC is deferred for hemorrhagic transformation, GI bleeding, thrombocytopenia or procedures, the summary should state the rationale and a review/restart plan.

Apixaban

Do not reduce dose for age alone

Apixaban 2.5 mg BD requires two dose-reduction criteria. Under the ≤60 kg sensitivity assumption, 7 prescriptions still look potentially under-dosed.

Valvular AF

Rheumatic MS is a separate pathway

Moderate/severe rheumatic MS with AF should trigger VKA-based anticoagulation planning rather than routine DOAC selection.

Audit documentation

Make the discharge summary auditable

Record AF type/status, stroke-risk score, anticoagulant + dose, renal function, bleeding reason if withheld, and follow-up / restart instructions.

Interpretation & limitations

What this audit can say: it can identify documented stroke risk, documented discharge anticoagulation, dosing signals, bleeding signals and valvular exceptions.
What it cannot prove: absence of a risk factor, actual body weight, adherence, complete external history, exact mechanical-valve type, or whether a clinician intentionally deferred anticoagulation for a reason not recorded in the summary.
  • CHA₂DS₂-VASc and CHA₂DS₂-VA are documented minimum estimates derived from age, sex and text-supported CHF, HTN, DM, stroke/TIA and vascular disease.
  • Creatinine is linked from serial investigations and reported as the latest available value around the admission period; 42/55 admissions had a usable latest creatinine.
  • Weight ≤60 kg is an explicit audit assumption for the apixaban sensitivity analysis. Actual dose adjudication requires measured weight and renal-function review.
  • Bleeding flags are conservative text signals. Hemorrhagic transformation and active GI bleeding may justify temporary withholding, but the optimal restart date requires case-specific review.
  • “Possible treatment gap” means manual review required, not confirmed inappropriate care.

Guideline anchors used for interpretation

  • 2024 ESC Guidelines for the management of atrial fibrillation — AF-CARE, stroke prevention, reassessment.
  • 2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS Guideline for atrial fibrillation — thromboembolic risk–based anticoagulation.
  • ACC: Appropriate dosing of novel anticoagulants — apixaban dose-reduction criteria.
