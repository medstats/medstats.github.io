Clinical Audit: Diabetes Discharge Care

Audit Overview

This dashboard presents a retrospective clinical quality audit of 57 inpatients diagnosed with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) discharged from the Department of General Medicine at Santosh Medical College and Hospital, Ghaziabad.

The audit evaluated documentation completeness against a 10-parameter diabetes discharge-care rubric, focusing heavily on patient-safety issues including Urine Albumin Creatinine Ratio (UACR) screening, renal suitability of antidiabetic drugs, and unsafe sulfonylurea use.

Key Diagnostic & Screening Insights

Urine Albumin Creatinine Ratio (UACR) Screening Rate: 31.6%
UACR screening for diabetic kidney disease was documented in only 18 out of 57 patients. This represents a significant missed opportunity for early detection and initiation of cardiorenal protective therapies (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors or RAS blockers).
Inappropriate Renal Prescribing & Safety Flags: 14.0%
A total of 8 patients were identified with inappropriate antidiabetic drug selections at discharge, including Dapagliflozin/Metformin prescribed in severe acute kidney injury (AKI) or GFR < 30, and Pioglitazone prescribed in an active pelvic fracture.

Materials and Methods
1

Audit cohort & setting

A retrospective clinical audit of 57 patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) discharged from the Department of General Medicine at Santosh Medical College and Hospital, Ghaziabad during the June–July 2026 clinical periods.

2

Data extraction pipeline

Discharge summaries were filtered using standard diagnostic keywords (T2DM, T2 DM, Diabetes Mellitus). PDF texts were parsed page-by-page using Poppler-based pdftotext. Preliminary auditing was conducted by six parallel AI subagents running Gemini 3.5 Flash and consolidated into a unified clinical database.

3

Audit criteria

The audit graded 10 distinct care items including HbA1c documentation, hypoglycemia risk screening, insulin/OHA reconciliation, renal appropriateness of drug selection, self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) instructions, hypoglycemia safety education, and follow-up plans.

4

UACR & OHA class profiling

Urine Albumin Creatinine Ratio (UACR) screening rates were determined by scanning investigation databases in text. Antidiabetic oral medications were profiled using the local drug brand mapping directory (kb.txt) to resolve brand names (e.g. Ondero Met, Trajenta Duo, Jalra-M, Glycomet GP) into standardized drug classes.

Diabetes Discharge Quality Improvement Plan

Immediate

Standardized Discharge Templates

Implement mandatory checkboxes in discharge summaries for Hypoglycemia Warning Signs education and Sick-Day Rules (dose holds during fever/dehydration).

Immediate

eGFR-based Prescribing Alerts

Integrate automated electronic medical record alerts restricting Metformin and SGLT2 inhibitors when discharge serum creatinine exceeds 1.4 mg/dL or during active AKI.

High Priority

Routine UACR Screening

Establish a protocol requiring spot Urine Albumin Creatinine Ratio (UACR) screening for all diabetic admissions to optimize cardiorenal drug selection (ACEi/ARB/SGLT2i).

High Priority

Sulfonylurea Deprescribing

Restrict Glimepiride at discharge in patients aged ≥ 65 years or those admitted with sepsis/shock; recommend transitioning to Linagliptin or basal insulin.

High Priority

SMBG Tracking Instructions

Define exact home blood glucose monitoring frequencies (e.g. twice daily, fasting & post-meal) instead of generic "monitor blood glucose" statements.

Immediate

Post-Hypoglycemia Safety holds

Mandate a 2-week hold on all sulfonylureas for patients admitted with hypoglycemia, with mandatory short-term General Medicine OPD review within 5 days.
