Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Classic CSF Formula of TBM: A subacute presentation of lymphocytic pleocytosis (100–500 cells/µL), profound hypoglycorrhachia (CSF glucose <20 mg/dL, CSF:serum ratio <0.2), and marked protein elevation (>200 mg/dL) is pathognomonic for Tuberculous Meningitis.
  2. Empiric ATT Must Not Await Rapid PCR: Standard GeneXpert Ultra and AFB smears have only 50–70% sensitivity in CSF; a negative rapid test must NEVER delay initiating empiric 4-drug Anti-Tubercular Therapy (ATT) when the CSF formula is suggestive.
  3. Essential Role of Adjunctive Corticosteroids: High-dose adjunctive Dexamethasone (0.4 mg/kg/day with slow 6–8 week taper) reduces mortality and prevents progressive basilar arachnoiditis in TBM.
  4. Basilar Vasculitis and Perforator Infarction: Thick gelatinous tuberculous exudates encase the circle of Willis, producing basilar arteritis and perforator strokes (middle cerebral and brainstem arteries)—the primary cause of permanent neurological morbidity.
  5. Adjunctive Aspirin for Stroke Prevention: Adding low-dose Aspirin (100–150 mg daily) alongside high-dose steroids and ATT helps mitigate basilar vasculitis and reduces ischemic stroke risk during the critical 1–3 week window.
  6. Severe Intracranial Hypertension (Opening Pressure >50 cm H2O): TBM frequently produces severe obstructive or communicating hydrocephalus; urgent CSF diversion via External Ventricular Drain (EVD) or lumbar drain is life-saving.
  7. Diagnostic Utility of CSF mNGS: Metagenomic Next-Generation Sequencing (mNGS) detects cell-free Mycobacterium tuberculosis DNA in culture-negative or PCR-negative CSF, serving as a powerful diagnostic tool for complex meningoencephalitis.
  8. Parahippocampal Tuberculoma Mimicry: Early parenchymal tuberculomas located in the parahippocampal or temporal regions can produce FLAIR hyperintensity on MRI, frequently misdiagnosed as HSV encephalitis.
  9. Role of Susceptibility (SWI/GRE) MRI: Hemorrhagic components within tuberculomas or vasculitic infarcts are best visualized using Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI) or Gradient Recalled Echo (GRE) MRI sequences.
  10. Blood-Brain Barrier Penetration of Second-Line Agents: In severe TBM with coma or vasculitis, adding CNS-penetrant second-line agents (such as Linezolid or Moxifloxacin) to standard HRZE improves early CSF bactericidal activity.
  11. Hyponatremia Management (SIADH vs. CSW): TBM patients frequently develop severe hyponatremia due to Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW) or SIADH; CSW requires hypertonic saline and fludrocortisone rather than fluid restriction.
  12. Temporary Sensorium Clearing after EVD Placement: EVD placement produces transient clinical improvement by reducing intracranial pressure, but this must not be mistaken for resolution of underlying active mycobacterial infection.
  13. High-Volume Centrifuged CSF Sampling: Sending large-volume (10–15 mL) centrifuged CSF samples for repeat Xpert Ultra and liquid MGIT cultures significantly increases diagnostic yield.
  14. Paradoxical Tuberculous Reactions: Clinical or radiologic worsening weeks after starting effective ATT (paradoxical reaction) represents delayed hypersensitivity rather than treatment failure and requires intensified corticosteroid therapy.
  15. Mandatory Household Contact Tracing: Confirmation of CNS tuberculosis requires immediate contact investigation with IGRA/TST and chest radiographs for all family and household contacts.
