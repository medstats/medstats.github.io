Best current diagnosis: Acute gastrointestinal illness causing severe dehydration and fluid-responsive hypovolemic shock. However, “gastroenteritis” does not explain the anemia and leukopenia, which require independent evaluation; a definitive unifying diagnosis is not yet established.

Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Think of cyclic neutropenia in any young adult with recurrent oral ulcers, episodic abdominal pain, and repeated infections. This classic triad should trigger serial CBCs rather than episode-based treatment.
  2. Recurrent "gastroenteritis" with hypotension is a diagnostic trap. Anchor away from a single-admission label; the pattern across admissions is what matters.
  3. Always calculate the ANC yourself. Manual calculation (TLC × % neutrophils) caught an error here (reported 3,000 vs. true ~1,550–1,950/µL) and grades severity objectively.
  4. Severe neutropenia is defined as ANC <500/µL. An ANC of 40/µL here is profound agranulocytosis and mandates febrile-neutropenia protocols.
  5. Positive serologies (dengue, brucella) do not equal the diagnosis. Dengue with a normal platelet count and brucellosis without exposure were red herrings; confirm with titers, PCR, or cultures.
  6. Preserved platelets and other cell lines point away from aplastic anemia, MDS, and leukemia. Isolated cyclical neutropenia with rebound recovery is the hallmark.
  7. Bone marrow at the trough shows myeloid maturation arrest. Timing matters — a marrow done away from the nadir may look normal and mislead.
  8. ~21-day periodicity is the single most diagnostic feature. Document it with CBCs 2–3 times weekly over 6–8 weeks rather than relying on recall.
  9. Cyclosporin is not justified without evidence of immune-mediated disease. Negative autoantibody panels and a cyclical pattern favour an intrinsic (genetic) defect.
  10. G-CSF (filgrastim) is the mainstay of cyclic neutropenia. The goal is to raise the nadir (ANC >0.5–1.0 ×10³/µL) and prevent severe infections — not to normalise every count.
  11. Every fever in a neutropenic patient is an emergency. Immediate ED evaluation and empiric antipseudomonal antibiotics, with a home fever action card.
  12. NGS confirmed the ELANE gene mutation. ELANE testing (plus the congenital-neutropenia panel) is confirmatory and distinguishes cyclic neutropenia from severe congenital neutropenia.
  13. Genetic counselling and cascade testing of first-degree relatives are essential. ELANE mutations are autosomal dominant.
  14. MDS/AML surveillance should be individualised. Risk of leukemic evolution is chiefly in the severe congenital neutropenia end of the spectrum; classic cyclic neutropenia carries substantially lower risk.
  15. Supportive care closes the loop: dental/oral hygiene, pneumococcal/Hib/influenza vaccines, and avoiding unnecessary immunosuppression reduce morbidity between nadirs.
