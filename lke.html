Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Leukoerythroblastosis Signifies Marrow Infiltration: The presence of nucleated red blood cells, immature granulocytes (metamyelocytes, myelocytes), and teardrop cells (dacrocytes) on a peripheral blood smear is the pathognomonic hallmark of myelophthisis (space-occupying bone marrow infiltration).
  2. Distinguishing Myelophthisis from TTP: While red cell fragmentation can occur from tumor microthrombi in widespread metastasis, a normal serum haptoglobin (91 mg/dL) and normal indirect bilirubin (0.7 mg/dL) definitively dismantle a primary TTP diagnosis.
  3. Pitfalls of Diagnostic Anchoring: Clinicians must independently review blood smears and hemolysis kinetics when evaluating outside transfers; accepting a subjective "schistocyte" report without verifying microangiopathic hemolysis leads to inappropriate plasma exchange.
  4. Extreme Ferritin as a Biomarker of Marrow Disruption: Astronomical serum ferritin surges (>20,000 µg/L) alongside severe LDH elevation (>2,500 U/L) in an older adult reflect extensive bone marrow destruction and secondary hyperinflammation rather than isolated iron overload.
  5. Axial Back Pain and Acute Collapse in Cancer: New-onset midline spine pain culminating in acute immobility or floor collapse in a patient with constitutional B-symptoms represents a spinal cord compression or pathological fracture emergency until excluded by MRI.
  6. Inaccuracy of Plain Radiographs for Bone Metastases: Normal spine radiographs do not rule out osteolytic metastases or marrow infiltration, as plain X-rays require over 30–50% bone mineral loss before lytic lesions become radiographically apparent.
  7. SCLC as the Great Hematologic Mimicker: Small-Cell Lung Carcinoma (SCLC) is notorious for early, occult bone marrow infiltration and can present primarily as fulminant myelophthisic pancytopenia before a dominant lung primary is imaged.
  8. Bone Marrow Biopsy Diagnostic Gold Standard: Performing a bone marrow trephine biopsy with immunohistochemistry is essential in unexplained leukoerythroblastic pancytopenia, revealing sheets of malignant neuroendocrine tumor cells replacing normal marrow space.
  9. Neuroendocrine Immunohistochemical Panel: Malignant small-cell neuroendocrine carcinoma cells characteristically stain positive for CD56, Synaptophysin, and Chromogranin, while testing negative for lymphoid markers (CD3, CD20).
  10. Lymphangitic Carcinomatosis on Chest CT: High-resolution CT chest demonstrating nodular interlobular septal thickening, mediastinal/hilar lymphadenopathy, and small lung nodules reflects subpleural lymphangitic tumor dissemination.
  11. Secondary Pancreatic Metastases and Elevated Lipase: Marked serum lipase elevations (>3,000 U/L) and epigastric pain in metastatic SCLC result from direct parenchymal tumor deposits in the pancreas, which can mimic primary pancreatic disease.
  12. High Risk of Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage: Severe myelophthisic thrombocytopenia (platelets ≤10,000/µL) predisposes patients to spontaneous, life-threatening mucosal bleeding, requiring prophylactic acid suppression and aggressive platelet transfusion support.
  13. Contraindication to Cytotoxic Chemotherapy in Severe Myelophthisis: When bone marrow architecture is extensively destroyed by tumor infiltration (≥80% cellularity), first-line platinum-based chemotherapy is contraindicated due to prohibitive risk of fatal marrow aplasia.
  14. Early Goals-of-Care and Palliative Co-Management: Establishing clear, goal-concordant advance directives early in fulminant metastatic solid tumors prevents futile invasive procedures and ensures a peaceful transition to comfort-focused hospice care.
  15. Role of Autopsy in Resolving Diagnostic Paradoxes: Post-mortem examination confirms multi-organ metastatic burden (vertebrae, liver, pancreas, adrenals) and provides definitive clinicopathologic closure for complex paraneoplastic presentations.
