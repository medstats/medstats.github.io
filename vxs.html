Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Definition of VEXAS Syndrome: VEXAS (Vacuoles, E1 enzyme, X-linked, Autoinflammatory, Somatic UBA1 mutation) is an acquired hemato-inflammatory disorder of older men caused by somatic mutations in the UBA1 gene on the X-chromosome.
  2. Macrocytic Anemia as a Diagnostic Clue: Unexplained macrocytic anemia (MCV >100 fL) in an older male presenting with treatment-refractory "rheumatologic" disease or steroid-dependent fevers is the primary clue leading to VEXAS.
  3. Pathognomonic Bone Marrow Vacuoles: Bone marrow aspirate demonstrating characteristic cytoplasmic vacuoles in early myeloid and erythroid precursor cells (vacuolated myeloblasts and pronormoblasts) is the diagnostic morphological hallmark.
  4. Relapsing Chondritis Phenocopy: VEXAS frequently presents with painful auricular or nasal chondritis, often misdiagnosed as classic relapsing polychondritis before hematologic abnormalities emerge.
  5. Neutrophilic Dermatosis and Urticarial Lesions: Cutaneous manifestations include Sweet's-like neutrophilic dermatosis, tender violaceous plaques, pustules, and deep subcutaneous nodules that flare upon steroid tapering.
  6. High Prevalence of Thromboembolism: Venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism) occurs in over 50% of VEXAS patients due to massive systemic autoinflammation and hypercoagulability.
  7. Severe Corticosteroid Dependence: Patients demonstrate profound steroid dependence, flaring consistently whenever prednisone/prednisolone is weaned below 10–15 mg/day, while failing standard synthetic or biologic DMARDs.
  8. Sensorineural Hearing Loss and Neuropathy: Cranial nerve and peripheral nervous system involvement can produce sudden sensorineural hearing loss and mononeuritis multiplex (e.g. foot drop).
  9. Somatic vs. Germline Genetics: UBA1 mutations occur somatically in hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells; germline DNA testing (e.g. skin fibroblasts or buccal swab) is negative, distinguishing VEXAS from inherited immunodeficiencies.
  10. Targeted JAK Inhibition (Ruxolitinib): Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors, particularly Ruxolitinib, effectively suppress monocyte-driven NLRP3 inflammasome activation and facilitate corticosteroid tapering.
  11. Allogeneic HSCT as the Sole Curative Modality: Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (allo-HSCT) is the only known curative treatment that replaces the mutated somatic UBA1 hematopoietic clone.
  12. Overlap with Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS): Approximately 30–50% of VEXAS patients meet formal criteria for Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS), requiring risk stratification with myeloid NGS panels (e.g. TET2, DNMT3A).
  13. Marked Hyperferritinemia: Extreme elevations in serum ferritin (>900–2,000 ng/mL) without overt iron overload reflect severe innate immune system activation and monocyte hyperinflammation.
  14. Sterile Vasoplegic Inflammatory Shock: Severe VEXAS flares can present with sterile, culture-negative vasoplegic shock requiring ICU admission and high-dose IV corticosteroids to control hyperinflammation.
  15. Indefinite Anticoagulation Requirement: Given the exceptionally high risk of recurrent arterial and venous thrombosis, long-term therapeutic anticoagulation (e.g. DOACs or Warfarin) is indicated.
