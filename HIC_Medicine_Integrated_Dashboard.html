What HICC should see first

Respiratory sampling quality is the clearest diagnostic-stewardship signal.
Sputum shows 96 commensal/mixed-growth admissions among 105 tested (91.4%), while ET/tracheal specimens have a much higher documented pathogen yield (11/30, 36.7%).

These are not formal VAP/HAI rates. ET/tracheal isolates need clinical adjudication for infection versus colonisation.

Priority workstreams

  1. Sputum quality: specimen-quality criteria, collection training, microbiology rejection policy and monthly feedback.
  2. Blood-culture quality: move from admission-level “commensal” to set-level contamination monitoring.
  3. Urine diagnostic stewardship: review indications and avoid cultures for asymptomatic bacteriuria/non-specific pyuria.
  4. MDRO respiratory surveillance: unit/time clustering of CRAB, Klebsiella and Pseudomonas.
  5. 48–72 h antibiotic review: combine culture result + trajectory of CRP/PCT + clinical response.

Immediate

Standardise sputum and blood-culture collection processes; publish monthly quality metrics.

30–60 days

Create unit-specific antibiogram/MDRO line-list and prospective 48–72 h stewardship review.

Quarterly

Trend culture yield, contamination, Watch/Reserve exposure and ICU/HDU broad-spectrum use.

Stewardship interpretation

Broad-spectrum intensity is concentrated in higher-acuity care. ICU/HDU use is higher for meropenem ± sulbactam, piperacillin-tazobactam/cefoperazone-sulbactam, anti-MRSA agents and ceftazidime-avibactam. Ward care relies more heavily on ceftriaxone/cefotaxime and macrolides.

This is exposure during admission, not DOT/DDD and not necessarily empiric therapy. It cannot distinguish escalation from de-escalation.

Recommended monthly AMS–HICC metrics

  • Watch/Reserve exposure by ward and ICU/HDU
  • Carbapenem starts per 100 admissions
  • 48–72 h documented review after culture result
  • Culture-before-antibiotic rate in sepsis/ICU pneumonia
  • Oral step-down and discharge antibiotic rate
  • Sputum commensal/poor-quality rate
  • Blood-culture single-set and contamination rate
  • MDRO incidence by unit and specimen type
Single-dashboard principle: HICC and AMS should share the same diagnostic and prescribing denominators. A fall in broad-spectrum use is meaningful only if culture quality and infection outcomes are tracked alongside it.

Important limitations

  • Discharge-summary rows are retained to reproduce the supplied ICU/HDU-vs-Ward code denominators; some IP numbers may repeat.
  • Culture “pathogen positivity” is the minimum documented yield, not a complete laboratory information system denominator.
  • Blood contamination requires set-level definitions; the current commensal figure is not a formal contamination rate.
  • ET/tracheal positivity does not equal VAP; colonisation is possible.
  • Antibiotic exposure from TREATMENT GIVEN cannot determine exact timing, DOT, DDD or de-escalation.

Phenotype and statistical approach

Syndromes are transparent regex phenotypes using diagnosis plus hospital course. Biomarker comparison reduces serial tests to first and peak admission values and uses a two-sided Mann–Whitney U test. Culture categories are admission-level unions across available structured microbiology and narrative extraction.
