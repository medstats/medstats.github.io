Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Definition of Paraneoplastic CNS Vasculitis: Paraneoplastic Central Nervous System (CNS) vasculitis is an ultra-rare, immune-mediated inflammatory arteriopathy of small and medium cerebral vessels triggered by an underlying systemic malignancy, independent of direct tumor metastasis or carcinomatous meningitis.
  2. Primary Malignancy Association: Lung adenocarcinoma (particularly Stage IV metastatic disease) is the most frequent solid tumor associated with paraneoplastic CNS vasculitis, followed by hematologic malignancies (lymphomas and leukemias).
  3. Refractoriness to Therapeutic Anticoagulation: Recurrent multifocal ischemic strokes that continue to break through full-dose therapeutic anticoagulation (Apixaban, LMWH, IV Heparin) indicate an underlying inflammatory or paraneoplastic etiology rather than simple thromboembolism.
  4. Distinction from Trousseau Syndrome & NBTE: While Trousseau syndrome and non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis (NBTE) present with hypercoagulability and sterile vegetations, paraneoplastic CNS vasculitis features true inflammatory mural vessel destruction, though both can coexist in cancer patients.
  5. CSF Lymphocytic Pleocytosis & Protein Elevation: CSF analysis demonstrates mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein (e.g., 84–92 mg/dL) with normal glucose and negative cytology, serving as a vital diagnostic clue for CNS inflammation over pure thrombosis.
  6. Diagnostic Significance of Elevated D-Dimer: Markedly elevated D-dimer levels (>5,000–9,000 ng/mL) in patients presenting with cryptogenic stroke mandate urgent thoracic, abdominal, and pelvic CT imaging to screen for occult malignancy.
  7. Progressive Leukoencephalopathy Pattern: Serial brain MRI demonstrating progressive, confluent periventricular and subcortical white matter T2/FLAIR hyperintensities alongside scattered acute DWI micro-infarcts reflects widespread cerebral microvascular inflammation and ischemic injury.
  8. Limitations of Cerebral Angiography (DSA): Normal or non-diagnostic digital subtraction angiography (DSA) and vessel-wall MRI do not exclude small-vessel paraneoplastic CNS vasculitis, as vessels <500 µm are beyond the spatial resolution of conventional catheter angiography.
  9. Autopsy Histopathologic Hallmark: Post-mortem brain histology confirms mural T-cell (CD3+) and macrophage (CD68+) predominant perivascular and transmural lymphocytic infiltrates with vessel wall destruction and luminal occlusion.
  10. Absence of Parenchymal Tumor Infiltration: A defining pathological criterion for paraneoplastic CNS vasculitis is the complete absence of metastatic tumor cells, carcinomatous meningitis, or intravascular lymphoma within cerebral tissue.
  11. Mechanism of Molecular Mimicry & Cytokine Surge: Tumor-derived antigens and circulating pro-inflammatory cytokines trigger immune complex deposition or cell-mediated cross-reactivity against cerebral vascular endothelial antigens.
  12. High-Dose Corticosteroids & Immunosuppression: Management requires immediate initiation of high-dose pulse corticosteroids (IV Methylprednisolone 1g daily) combined with targeted immunosuppressive therapy to halt progressive neurovascular destruction.
  13. Primacy of Systemic Tumor Control: Long-term resolution of paraneoplastic CNS vasculitis is contingent upon prompt, aggressive oncologic therapy (chemotherapy, targeted TKIs, or immunotherapy) to eliminate the antigenic source.
  14. Pitfall of Anchoring on Atrial Fibrillation: Co-existing atrial fibrillation often acts as a diagnostic red herring, causing clinicians to misattribute recurrent strokes to cardioembolism and delay cancer screening.
  15. Systemic ESUS Workup Protocol: In any patient with embolic stroke of undetermined source (ESUS) accompanied by CSF inflammation, elevated D-dimer, or anticoagulation failure, immediate body CT and tissue biopsy are mandatory to identify occult malignancy.
