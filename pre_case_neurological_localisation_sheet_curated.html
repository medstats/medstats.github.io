Pre-Case Neurological Localisation Sheet

Anatomical structure → history clues → examination clues
Curated by Anupam Singh MD, Professor, Department of Medicine, Santosh Medical College
Use before solving cases: identify the anatomical level first. Ask what symptoms should be present if that structure is involved, then deliberately look for the matching positive examination signs — and for important findings that should be absent.

Cerebrum & deep structures
Anatomical structure / localisation Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit
Dominant frontal cortex — Broca areaDifficulty getting words out; short effortful speech; patient appears to know what they want to say; writing may also be impaired.Nonfluent/effortful speech; impaired naming and repetition; comprehension relatively preserved; right lower facial/arm weakness may coexist.
Dominant posterior temporal cortex — Wernicke areaFluent but meaningless speech; difficulty understanding conversation; family may report inappropriate answers.Fluent paraphasic speech; poor comprehension; impaired repetition/naming; possible right superior quadrantanopia.
Dominant parietal cortexDifficulty calculating/writing; right-left confusion; cannot recognise objects by touch; difficulty performing learned acts.Acalculia, agraphia, finger agnosia, right-left disorientation; ideomotor apraxia; astereognosis/agraphesthesia.
Nondominant parietal cortexIgnores one side; leaves food on one half of plate; bumps into objects on one side; poor spatial awareness.Contralateral neglect; extinction on double simultaneous stimulation; constructional apraxia; anosognosia.
Medial frontal cortex / ACA territoryLeg-predominant weakness; reduced initiative/abulia; urinary urgency or incontinence.Contralateral leg weakness > arm; hyperreflexia/Babinski; grasp reflex; frontal release signs.
Frontal eye fieldEyes noticed deviated to one side after acute event.Conjugate gaze deviation toward cortical lesion and away from hemiparesis.
Supplementary motor area / medial frontal networkDifficulty initiating movement; limb may act independently; intermanual conflict.Grasp reflex; alien-hand phenomena; impaired motor initiation; possible leg-predominant weakness.
Bilateral frontal operculaCannot voluntarily speak, swallow, cough or move lower face/tongue, but spontaneous emotional movements may persist.Automatic-voluntary dissociation; severe voluntary faciopharyngoglossal weakness with preserved spontaneous smiling/yawning.
Occipital cortexLoss of one side of visual world; bumping into objects; no ocular pain.Contralateral homonymous hemianopia; highly congruous defect; normal pupils/fundi; macular sparing may occur.
Bilateral occipital cortexBehaves blind; may deny blindness or confabulate visual descriptions.Cortical blindness with preserved pupillary reflexes and normal fundi; Anton syndrome if denial/confabulation.
Parieto-occipital association cortex — bilateralSees one object at a time; cannot take in a whole scene; misses objects when reaching.Simultanagnosia + optic ataxia + ocular apraxia — Bálint syndrome.
Fusiform / inferior occipitotemporal cortexCannot recognise familiar faces but recognises people by voice, gait or clothing.Prosopagnosia with preserved visual acuity and object perception.
Hippocampus / medial temporal lobesRepeated questions; poor memory for recent events; remote memory relatively better.Marked anterograde memory impairment; impaired delayed recall/new learning; attention and language may be preserved.
Bilateral anterior temporal lobes / amygdalaHyperorality, hypersexuality, loss of fear, excessive visual exploration, behavioural change.Klüver-Bucy pattern: placidity, hypermetamorphosis, visual agnosia, memory disturbance.
Internal capsuleSudden dense face-arm-leg weakness with little/no cortical symptoms.Pure motor hemiparesis; UMN face/arm/leg signs; hyperreflexia/Babinski; no aphasia/neglect/visual field defect.
ThalamusSudden hemisensory numbness; later burning pain/allodynia may appear.Contralateral face-arm-leg sensory loss; power relatively preserved; hyperpathia/allodynia; cheiro-oral pattern with small lesions.
Subthalamic nucleusSudden violent flinging movements of one side.Contralateral hemiballismus; large-amplitude ballistic proximal movements.
Basal ganglia / substantia nigraSlowness; rest tremor; reduced arm swing; softer voice; micrographia.Bradykinesia, rigidity, rest tremor, masked facies, reduced arm swing, postural instability.
Temporal optic radiation — Meyer loopDifficulty noticing objects in opposite upper visual quadrant.Contralateral superior quadrantanopia — 'pie in the sky'.
Parietal optic radiationDifficulty noticing objects in opposite lower visual quadrant.Contralateral inferior quadrantanopia — 'pie on the floor'; parietal signs may coexist.

Cerebellum & ataxia
Anatomical structure / localisation Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit
Cerebellar hemisphereClumsy same-side limb; spills drinks; overshoots targets; veers toward affected side.Ipsilateral dysmetria, dysdiadochokinesia, intention tremor, rebound, heel-shin impairment.
Cerebellar vermisPredominantly difficulty standing/walking; truncal swaying; little hand clumsiness.Broad-based gait; truncal titubation; impaired tandem gait; limb coordination relatively preserved.
Sensory proprioceptive pathwayUnsteadiness worse in dark; needs to watch feet; stamping gait.Loss of vibration/joint position; positive Romberg; pseudoathetosis; worsens on eye closure.

Visual pathway
Anatomical structure / localisation Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit
Optic nerveMonocular visual loss; colour desaturation; central blur; pain on eye movement if optic neuritis.Reduced acuity; RAPD; impaired colour vision; central scotoma; disc may initially be normal.
Optic chiasmProgressive peripheral visual loss; bumping into door frames; endocrine symptoms may coexist.Bitemporal hemianopia; optic pallor later; ocular motility often initially normal.
Optic tractSame visual hemifield lost in both eyes, often with deep cerebral symptoms.Contralateral homonymous hemianopia; possible contralateral RAPD; associated thalamic/internal capsular signs.

Cranial nerves, brainstem & skull base
Anatomical structure / localisation Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit
CN III — oculomotor nervePtosis, diplopia, eye turned outward; ask about sudden severe headache.Ptosis; eye down and out; impaired adduction/elevation/depression; pupil involvement suggests compression.
CN IV — trochlear nerveVertical diplopia worse while reading or walking downstairs; compensatory head tilt.Hypertropia; weak depression of adducted eye; diplopia worsens with head tilt toward affected side.
CN VI — abducens nerveHorizontal diplopia worse looking toward affected side.Failure of ipsilateral abduction; isolated palsy = nerve/fascicle; both eyes fail to gaze toward side = VI nucleus/PPRF.
MLFHorizontal diplopia; commonly demyelination in younger patients or stroke in older patients.INO: impaired adduction on side of lesion + abducting nystagmus of opposite eye; convergence often preserved.
PPRF / abducens nucleusCannot look toward one side with either eye.Ipsilateral conjugate horizontal gaze palsy; vertical gaze preserved.
Dorsal rostral midbrainDifficulty looking upward; diplopia; headache/hydrocephalus symptoms may coexist.Vertical gaze palsy; convergence-retraction nystagmus; light-near dissociation; lid retraction.
CN V — trigeminalFacial numbness/pain; chewing difficulty.V1/V2/V3 sensory loss; absent afferent corneal reflex; weak masseter/pterygoid; jaw deviates toward weak side.
CN VII — supranuclearSudden mouth deviation often with limb weakness.Contralateral lower facial weakness with forehead sparing; eye closure preserved; accompanying UMN signs.
CN VII — peripheral / LMNWhole side of face weak; cannot close eye; food/water leaks; ask taste, hyperacusis, lacrimation.Ipsilateral upper + lower facial paralysis; proximal lesions may add taste loss, hyperacusis and reduced lacrimation.
CN VIII / vestibular pathwayVertigo, oscillopsia, nausea; hearing loss/tinnitus suggests cochlear/AICA/CPA involvement.Nystagmus; gait veering; head impulse abnormal in peripheral lesions; sensorineural hearing loss if cochlear component.
CN IX/X / nucleus ambiguusDysphagia, nasal regurgitation, choking, weak cough, hoarseness.Palatal droop; uvula deviates away; reduced gag; dysphonia; weak cough; vocal-cord paralysis.
CN XIShoulder droop; difficulty shrugging or turning head; may follow neck surgery.Trapezius wasting/weak shrug; SCM weakness causing weak head rotation to opposite side.
CN XII — LMNDysarthria; difficulty manipulating food in mouth.Ipsilateral tongue wasting/fasciculations; tongue deviates toward lesion on protrusion.
Midbrain — medial/ventralPtosis/diplopia with opposite body weakness.Ipsilateral III palsy + contralateral UMN weakness — Weber-type localisation.
Midbrain tegmentumIII-nerve symptoms plus opposite tremor/incoordination.Ipsilateral III palsy + contralateral tremor/dysmetria/ataxia — red nucleus/superior cerebellar pathway.
Medial ponsDiplopia or facial weakness plus opposite limb weakness.Ipsilateral VI and/or VII fascicular deficit + contralateral hemiparesis.
Dorsal pons / facial colliculusCannot look toward one side + same-sided complete facial weakness.Ipsilateral conjugate gaze palsy + ipsilateral LMN VII palsy; ± contralateral weakness.
Dorsal pons — PPRF + MLFSevere horizontal gaze difficulty.One-and-a-half syndrome: gaze palsy toward lesion + INO when looking away.
PPRF/VI nucleus + MLF + VII fascicleHorizontal gaze paralysis plus complete ipsilateral facial paralysis.Eight-and-a-half syndrome = one-and-a-half syndrome + VII palsy.
Lateral pons / AICA territoryVertigo, vomiting, ipsilateral hearing loss, facial weakness/numbness, gait unsteadiness.Ipsilateral LMN facial palsy/deafness/facial pain-temperature loss/ataxia + contralateral body pain-temperature loss.
Lateral medullaVertigo, vomiting, dysphagia, hoarseness, hiccups, gait instability.Ipsilateral facial pain-temperature loss + contralateral body pain-temperature loss; ipsilateral Horner/ataxia/palatal weakness.
Medial medullaOpposite-sided weakness with tongue difficulty.Ipsilateral LMN XII palsy + contralateral hemiparesis; contralateral proprioceptive loss may occur.
Bilateral ventral ponsUnable to move or speak but appears conscious.Quadriplegia, anarthria, impaired horizontal gaze with preserved consciousness, blinking and vertical gaze — locked-in syndrome.
Cerebellopontine angleProgressive unilateral hearing loss/tinnitus; later facial numbness and imbalance.Ipsilateral sensorineural deafness; absent corneal reflex; facial weakness; ipsilateral ataxia/nystagmus.
Cavernous sinusPainful ophthalmoplegia; retro-orbital headache; facial numbness.Combined III/IV/VI palsies + V1 ± V2 loss; optic nerve generally spared; VI + Horner particularly localising.
Orbital apexPainful visual loss plus diplopia/ptosis.Optic neuropathy/RAPD + III/IV/VI and V1 abnormalities; visual loss distinguishes from cavernous sinus.
Jugular foramenHoarseness + dysphagia + shoulder weakness.IX, X, XI deficits; palate/vocal cord + trapezius/SCM; XII spared — Vernet pattern.
Jugular foramen + hypoglossal regionDysphagia, hoarseness, shoulder weakness and tongue weakness.IX–X–XI–XII palsies — Collet-Sicard pattern.
Retroparotid/parapharyngeal space + sympathetic chainLower cranial-nerve symptoms plus ptosis/miosis.IX–X–XI–XII palsies + Horner syndrome — Villaret pattern.
Extensive unilateral skull baseSlowly accumulating multiple cranial-nerve symptoms on one side with surprisingly normal limbs.Multiple unilateral cranial neuropathies without long-tract signs — Garcin pattern.
Petrous apexChronic otitis/otorrhoea followed by deep facial/retro-orbital pain and diplopia.Ipsilateral VI palsy + trigeminal V1/V2 pain/sensory signs with ear infection — Gradenigo pattern.
Intramedullary cord lesionPoorly localised burning pain; gradual bilateral symptoms; bladder late.Dissociated sensory loss; sacral sparing; segmental LMN signs at level + long-tract signs below.
Extramedullary cord compressionLocal back pain → radicular pain → progressive leg weakness; sphincter symptoms late.Early root signs; asymmetric long-tract abnormalities; Brown-Séquard pattern; sensory level develops.
Conus medullarisSaddle numbness; very early urinary retention, bowel or sexual dysfunction; less radicular pain.Symmetric saddle anesthesia; reduced anal tone; absent anal wink/bulbocavernosus; mixed UMN/LMN leg signs.

Roots, plexus, nerve, NMJ & motor neuron
Anatomical structure / localisation Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit
Bilateral corticobulbar tractsDysarthria/dysphagia; inappropriate laughing/crying; multiple prior strokes may be present.Spastic dysarthria; brisk jaw jerk; exaggerated gag; slow stiff tongue without wasting; emotional lability.
Cervical enlargementHand/arm weakness/wasting plus stiff legs; hand clumsiness and gait difficulty.LMN signs in arms at lesion + UMN signs in legs below; sensory level; Hoffmann/Babinski may be present.
Anterior horn cellProgressive painless focal wasting/weakness without numbness.Segmental wasting, fasciculations, weakness and reduced reflexes; sensation normal.
Motor neuron system — UMN + LMNProgressive weakness spreading between regions; cramps/fasciculations; bulbar symptoms; no sensory complaints.Wasting/fasciculations + brisk reflexes/Babinski; tongue LMN signs possible; sensation and sphincters usually preserved.
Cervical nerve rootNeck pain radiating down arm; cough/sneeze may worsen; paresthesia in a dermatome.Myotomal weakness; reduced corresponding reflex; dermatomal sensory loss; Spurling may reproduce symptoms.
C6 rootPain/paresthesia lateral arm to thumb.Weak elbow flexion/wrist extension; reduced biceps/brachioradialis reflex; thumb/lateral forearm sensory loss.
C7 rootPosterior arm pain to middle finger.Weak triceps/finger extension/wrist flexion; reduced triceps reflex; middle-finger sensory loss.
C8 rootMedial forearm pain to ring/little fingers.Weak finger flexion/intrinsic hand muscles across median + ulnar nerves; C8 sensory pattern.
L5 rootBack pain radiating down lateral leg to dorsum of foot; foot drop.Weak dorsiflexion plus inversion and hip abduction; L5 sensory loss; ankle jerk usually preserved.
S1 rootBack pain radiating down posterior leg/lateral foot; difficulty standing on toes.Weak plantarflexion; reduced/absent ankle jerk; lateral-foot sensory loss; difficulty toe walking.
Lower trunk brachial plexus C8–T1Shoulder/arm pain followed by hand weakness; medial forearm/hand numbness.Weakness crossing median + ulnar territories; medial forearm sensory loss; Horner may coexist in Pancoast lesion.
Common peroneal nerveFoot drop after leg crossing, squatting or weight loss; usually no back pain.Weak dorsiflexion + eversion; inversion preserved; sensory loss anterolateral leg/dorsum foot; ankle jerk normal.
Sciatic nerveHip/buttock trauma or surgery followed by posterior-leg symptoms and foot weakness.Dorsiflexion and plantarflexion weak; inversion/eversion weak; ankle jerk reduced; hamstring involvement may occur.
Radial nerve — spiral grooveWrist drop after prolonged arm compression.Wrist/finger extension weakness; triceps preserved; small first dorsal web-space sensory deficit.
Posterior interosseous nerveFinger drop without numbness.Finger/thumb extension weak; wrist extension preserved but may deviate radially; no sensory loss.
Anterior interosseous nerveDifficulty pinching/picking up small objects; no numbness.Cannot make normal 'OK' sign; weak FPL and index FDP; pure motor lesion.
Neuromuscular junction — postsynapticFluctuating ptosis/diplopia; chewing/speech worsens with use; better after rest.Fatigable weakness; variable ophthalmoplegia; pupils normal; sensation normal; reflexes preserved.
Neuromuscular junction — presynapticProximal weakness improves after activity; dry mouth and autonomic symptoms; smoking history relevant.Reduced reflexes that augment after exercise; proximal weakness; autonomic signs.
Diffuse peripheral roots/nervesAscending weakness after infection; paresthesias; facial weakness; autonomic symptoms.Symmetric flaccid weakness + generalized areflexia; mild sensory deficit; facial diplegia; autonomic instability.

Spinal cord, conus & cauda
Anatomical structure / localisation Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit
High cervical cord C1–C4Neck pain; hand/leg weakness progressing to quadriparesis; breathing difficulty.UMN weakness in all four limbs; sensory level; diaphragmatic weakness at C3–5; cranial nerves normal.
C5–C6 cervical cordNeck/arm symptoms with progressive hand clumsiness and leg stiffness.Reduced biceps/brachioradialis reflex at level but brisk triceps/fingers/legs below; inverted supinator may occur.
Thoracic cordProgressive leg heaviness/stiffness; band-like truncal sensation; urinary urgency; arms unaffected.Spastic paraparesis; brisk knees/ankles; bilateral Babinski; truncal sensory level; arms normal.
Dorsal columnsUnsteadiness worse in dark; needs visual guidance; electric sensations possible.Loss of vibration/proprioception; positive Romberg; stamping gait; pseudoathetosis.
Spinothalamic tractPoor appreciation of pain/temperature; unnoticed burns/injuries.Contralateral pain-temperature loss below cord lesion, often starting a few segments below.
Anterior cordSudden severe weakness, often vascular/aortic setting; bladder involvement.Bilateral paralysis/UMN signs + pain-temperature loss; vibration and position preserved.
Posterior cordSevere sensory ataxia with relatively preserved strength and pain sensation.Loss of vibration/proprioception; positive Romberg; pain-temperature comparatively preserved.
HemicordTrauma/compression with unilateral weakness and sensory symptoms.Ipsilateral UMN weakness + ipsilateral vibration/proprioception loss + contralateral pain-temperature loss — Brown-Séquard.
Central cervical cord / syrinxBurns hands unknowingly; progressive hand weakness; cape-like numbness.Bilateral segmental pain-temperature loss with preserved vibration; hand wasting/areflexia; later UMN legs.
Acute transverse cord lesionRapid bilateral weakness; numbness rising to a level; urinary retention.Bilateral motor deficit + clear sensory level + autonomic dysfunction; initially flaccid, later spastic.
Spinal shockImmediately after severe acute spinal injury: complete paralysis below lesion.Flaccidity and areflexia below lesion despite cord disease; bulbocavernosus initially absent; later hyperreflexia/spasticity.
EpiconusDistal leg weakness prominent; sphincter symptoms less abrupt than conus.Mixed LMN distal legs + possible Babinski/UMN findings; ankle jerks often absent; bladder less dominant initially.
Cauda equinaSevere back/radicular leg pain; asymmetric weakness/numbness; bladder dysfunction often later.Asymmetric LMN weakness; reduced/absent reflexes; flexor plantars; patchy root sensory loss; saddle anesthesia.
