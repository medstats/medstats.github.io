Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Monocytopenia as a Diagnostic Red Flag: Absolute monocytopenia (<1% or <20/µL) in the setting of severe pancytopenia and splenomegaly is highly specific for Hairy-Cell Leukemia (HCL) and reflects profound cell-mediated immunodeficiency.
  2. Pathognomonic HCL Immunophenotype: Neoplastic B cells in classic HCL characteristically co-express CD19, CD20, CD11c, CD103, and CD25, accompanied by Annexin A1 and Tartrate-Resistant Acid Phosphatase (TRAP) positivity.
  3. "Dry Tap" Marrow Aspiration: Neoplastic hairy cell infiltration induces extensive marrow reticulin fibrosis, leading to an inability to aspirate bone marrow fluid ("dry tap") and necessitating core biopsy for diagnosis.
  4. The "Flip-Flop Fungus Sign" on PET-CT: On 18F-FDG PET-CT, mediastinal and supraclavicular lymph nodes showing markedly higher FDG hypermetabolism than the primary pulmonary nodule favor granulomatous infection (fungal or mycobacterial) over primary lung carcinoma.
  5. Opportunistic NTM Infections in HCL: Severe monocytopenia and dendritic cell depletion in HCL create profound susceptibility to opportunistic Nontuberculous Mycobacterial (NTM) infections, such as Mycobacterium chimaera or M. avium complex.
  6. Distinction from Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS): A post-antibiotic maculopapular exanthem sparing palms/soles with absent mucosal erosions, absent skin tenderness, and a negative Nikolsky sign rules out SJS/TEN in favor of a morbilliform drug eruption.
  7. Role of EBUS-Guided Lymph Node Biopsy: Endobronchial Ultrasound (EBUS) biopsy of FDG-avid mediastinal nodes with 16S rRNA PCR sequencing provides definitive microbiologic identification of fastidious NTM species like M. chimaera.
  8. Infection Control Precedes Purine Analog Chemotherapy: Initiating targeted antimycobacterial therapy to control active NTM infection prior to Cladribine (2-CDA) administration prevents life-threatening septic complications during post-chemotherapy neutropenia.
  9. Avoidance of Anti-CD20 Antibodies During Active NTM Infection: Rituximab is withheld during initial HCL induction in patients with active disseminated mycobacterial disease to avoid worsening humoral and B-cell immunodeficiency.
  10. Targeted Purine Analog Monotherapy (Cladribine): A single 5-to-7 day course of Cladribine monotherapy achieves complete hematologic remission rates exceeding 85–90% in classic Hairy-Cell Leukemia.
  11. Management of Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI): In NTM treatment, hepatotoxicity from Rifampin requires substitution with Rifabutin or Clofazimine alongside Macrolides and Ethambutol while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.
  12. Anergy on Interferon-Gamma Release Assays (IGRA): Unreactive positive controls on IGRA testing signal severe T-cell/monocyte-mediated immune suppression, cautioning against relying on blood IGRA for tuberculosis/NTM screening.
  13. BRAF V600E Molecular Profiling: Over 95% of classic HCL cases harbor the BRAF V600E somatic mutation; identifying BRAF wild-type disease prompts testing for MAP2K1 mutations or HCL-variant features.
  14. Histologic Resolution Monitoring: Serial CT/PET-CT imaging demonstrating complete disappearance of FDG-avid lymphadenopathy and pulmonary nodules confirms microbiologic cure of NTM infection.
  15. Long-Term Minimal Residual Disease (MRD) Surveillance: Given late relapse risks in HCL, patients require long-term CBC monitoring, periodic flow cytometry, and annual clinical evaluation.
