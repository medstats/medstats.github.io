Overview

This dashboard is designed for Departmental review. It focuses on volume, bed-days, length of stay, critical-care use, discharge outcomes, repeat utilisation, case mix and investigation workload. The source of data are individual Discharge Summaries with a human oversight.

Administrative interpretation

Bed pressure is better represented by bed-days than admission count alone. Long stays, high-acuity admissions and delays around transfer or discharge are the most actionable areas for monthly review. A true occupancy percentage cannot be calculated without the staffed-bed denominator.

Monthly LAMA, transfer and death

Disposition counts

Administrative use

LAMA is a useful operational and patient-safety signal because its burden is larger than inpatient mortality in this registry. It should be reviewed by diagnosis, unit, LOS, ICU/HDU status, payer/organisation and time of discharge. Transfers should be separated into planned referral versus inability to provide required service whenever the narrative supports that distinction.

Definition

Readmission means return to this hospital only. Each window uses only index discharges with enough observable follow-up before the dataset end date. Deaths and hospital transfers are excluded as index discharges.

Documentation completeness

Interpretation

Investigation groups are cleaned to simple labels such as CBC, KFT, LFT, CRP and PT/INR. Counts shown above are approximate test occasions, defined as one cleaned investigation group per admission-summary per reported date. This avoids inflating CBC/LFT/KFT counts from their multiple parameter rows. For clinical audits, serial results should still be summarized into admission-level features such as first, worst and last values.
