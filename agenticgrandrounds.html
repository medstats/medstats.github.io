Agentic Grand Rounds

Overview

Agentic Grand Rounds represents a frontier in medical education where multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) orchestration is used to simulate complex clinical reasoning. Each case is a staged discussion between specialized AI roles:

  • Gemini (Resident): Initial case analysis, differential diagnosis, and proposed investigations.
  • Grok (Attending): Critical appraisal of the resident's plan, Bayesian refinement, and focus on "don't miss" diagnoses.
  • ChatGPT (Expert): Unifying diagnosis, key pivot identification, and high-yield teaching pearls.

This "Human-in-the-loop" framework (supervised by Anupam Singh MD) aims to sharpen diagnostic precision and explore the nuances of medical logic in a transparent, collaborative environment.

  • The Eosinophilic Surge: Exertional Dyspnea, Anosmia, Migratory Pulmonary Opacities, and Cardiac Biomarker Elevation

    A 91-year-old male retired military veteran with a history of obstructive airway disease, coronary artery disease, and 30-pack-year smoking history presents with an 8-month history of progressive exertional dyspnea, 15.9 kg weight loss, chronic sinus congestion, rhinorrhea, and anosmia. Physical examination reveals severe diffuse pulmonary wheezing and exertional desaturation to 70% on room air. Laboratory evaluation reveals a massive leukocytosis (37,780/µL) driven by extreme blood eosinophilia (absolute eosinophil count 27,390/µL), elevated high-sensitivity troponin T (56 ng/L), NT-proBNP (4,300 pg/mL), elevated serum IgG (3,099 mg/dL), and positive Aspergillus fumigatus IgE, with normal serum vitamin B12 and tryptase levels. High-resolution CT chest reveals diffuse bronchial-wall thickening, mucus plugging, tree-in-bud opacities, subcarinal lymphadenopathy, and bilateral pleural effusions. A high-stakes diagnostic dissection navigating systemic hypereosinophilic syndromes, ANCA serology variants, clonal hematopoiesis, and targeted anti-IL-5 receptor alpha monoclonal antibody therapy.

  • The Aplastic Surge: Refractory Post-Transplant Anemia, Severe Reticulocytopenia, and Iron Overload

    A 74-year-old male retired consultant presents 3 months following orthotopic heart transplantation with progressive severe transfusion-dependent normocytic anemia (hemoglobin nadir 5.6 g/dL) and mild pancytopenia. Despite multiple immunosuppressive drug adjustments for presumed medication-induced myelosuppression, the anemia worsens, requiring 6 units of packed red blood cells. Laboratory evaluation reveals profound reticulocytopenia (<0.5%), normal serum haptoglobin (142 mg/dL), negative Coombs testing, a massive hypoxia-driven erythropoietin surge (465 mIU/mL), severe iron overload (transferrin saturation 93%, ferritin 1229 µg/L), and splenomegaly (16.7 cm). A high-stakes diagnostic dissection navigating post-transplant hypoproliferative cytopenias, selective maturation arrest, quantitative viral genomics, passive antibody replacement, and allograft preservation.

  • The Ascending Sensory Level: Refractory Immunotherapy, Normal Spine Imaging, and Central Cord Conduction Loss

    A 57-year-old woman with a past surgical history of gastric bypass 4 years prior and chronic low back pain presents with an 18-month progressive history of severe sensory loss, paresthesias, and quadriparesis (0-1/5 leg strength, 3/5 arm strength) resulting in loss of bowel/bladder control and wheelchair dependence. Presumptive diagnosis of chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP) led to 6 months of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) without response. Neurological examination reveals hyperreflexia (3+ reflexes) and a pinprick sensory level at T4, while electrophysiological studies show normal sensory action potentials, reduced motor amplitudes, and absent tibial somatosensory evoked potentials (P37 wave). Laboratory evaluation reveals normal vitamin B12, normal methylmalonic acid, mild leukopenia, and normocytic anemia. A high-stakes diagnostic dissection navigating metabolic tractopathies, competitive trace metal toxicity, occult enterocyte trapping, and targeted parenteral micronutrient rescue.

  • The Fragile Smear: Subacute Weight Loss, Solid-Food Dysphagia, Severe Pancytopenia, and Peripheral Red Cell Fragmentation

    An 80-year-old female retired church worker presents with a 6-week history of progressive fatigue, anorexia, 6 kg weight loss, fevers, solid-food dysphagia, and severe pancytopenia (hemoglobin 7.5 g/dL, WBC 2.74 x 10³/µL, platelets 43,000/µL). Examination of a peripheral blood smear reveals prominent red cell fragments and schistocytes, leading to an initial misdiagnosis of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) and initiation of plasma exchange. Laboratory testing reveals normal haptoglobin (113 mg/dL), normal bilirubin, a hypoproliferative reticulocyte index (0.65%), and normal ADAMTS13 activity (76%), while abdominopelvic CT demonstrates bilateral adnexal masses (4.8 cm and 6.3 cm). A high-stakes diagnostic dissection navigating pseudo-TTP, dysplastic smear signatures, extramedullary myeloid lesions, complex cytogenetics, and epigenetic targeted therapy.
