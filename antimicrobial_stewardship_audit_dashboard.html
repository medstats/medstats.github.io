Clinical Audit: Antimicrobial Stewardship in Admitted Patients

Audit Overview

This dashboard summarises a retrospective audit of 174 unique inpatient admissions from the Department of General Medicine. Clinical syndromes were assigned from the structured diagnosis field, while culture results and antibiotic treatment were extracted from the linked discharge-summary PDFs.

Antibacterial appropriateness was screened against ICMR syndrome-based guidance. Antibiotics were classified using the WHO AWaRe 2025 framework. Brand names were resolved using the hospital drug knowledge base.

Materials and Methods
1

Cohort and linkage

187 PDF files represented 174 unique IP numbers. Thirteen IP numbers had duplicate PDF versions; the latest filename timestamp was retained. All unique IP numbers matched the structured CSV.

2

Syndrome classification

The primary syndrome was assigned from the DIAGNOSIS field in discharge_sections.csv. Multiple syndrome tags were retained internally, while one primary syndrome was used for percentage reporting.

3

PDF extraction

Discharge PDFs were converted to layout-preserving text. Blood, urine, respiratory, CSF, fluid, stool, wound/swab and catheter-tip culture information was searched and linked by IP number.

4

Drug normalisation

Hospital brand names were mapped to molecules using kb.txt. Inpatient treatment and discharge advice were analysed separately before deduplicating admission-level exposure.

5

Appropriateness framework

Diagnosis, extracted antibiotics and culture results were compared with syndrome-based ICMR guidance. Screening categories were Appropriate, Partially appropriate, Potentially inappropriate and Indeterminate/not assessable.

6

WHO AWaRe

Antibacterial molecules/regimens were assigned to Access, Watch, Reserve or Not classified using the WHO 2025 AWaRe classification. Antifungals, antivirals and anti-TB drugs were excluded from AWaRe event counts.

Interpretation Limits
Screening audit—not a full prescription-chart audit
The discharge documents could not reliably verify exact antibiotic start/stop times, dose, renal adjustment, allergy, prior exposure, timing of cultures, local susceptibility, source control, DDD or days of therapy. “Potentially inappropriate” therefore means requires targeted chart review, not proven misconduct or negligence.

Antimicrobial Stewardship Quality-Improvement Plan

Immediate

Mandatory indication and review date

Require an infection syndrome, suspected source, antibiotic indication and 48–72-hour review/stop date for every systemic antibacterial order.

Immediate

Culture-before-antibiotic bundle

For sepsis, pyelonephritis, pneumonia requiring ICU care and suspected CRBSI, prompt appropriate cultures before the first dose when this does not delay resuscitation.

High Priority

Access-first pathways

Create syndrome order sets that default to Access agents when clinically suitable and require justification for Watch/Reserve escalation.

High Priority

Gastroenteritis restriction

Add criteria for when antibiotics are indicated in acute diarrhoeal illness and discourage routine fluoroquinolone/cephalosporin use.

Immediate

Culture-directed discharge review

At discharge, verify organism susceptibility, oral bioavailability, source penetration and planned duration—especially after resistant Gram-negative infection.

High Priority

Monthly feedback dashboard

Track culture-sent rate, Access share, Reserve starts, antibiotic-at-discharge rate and potentially inappropriate themes by unit and syndrome.
