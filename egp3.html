Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Triphasic Natural History of EGPA: Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA) typically unfolds in three phases: an initial allergic/prodromal phase (asthma, allergic rhinitis, sinusitis, anosmia), a hypereosinophilic tissue-infiltration phase, and a late systemic necrotizing vasculitic phase.
  2. Key Clinical Diagnostic Triad: In elderly patients mislabeled with "refractory COPD" or recurrent pneumonia, the co-occurrence of new-onset asthma, chronic sinus congestion with anosmia, and severe blood hypereosinophilia is diagnostic for EGPA.
  3. Extreme Blood Hypereosinophilia: EGPA can produce massive surges in absolute eosinophil count (AEC >25,000/µL, >70% differential), reflecting intense interleukin-5 (IL-5)-mediated eosinophil proliferation and survival.
  4. Eosinophilic Myocarditis as the Primary Mortality Driver: Cardiac involvement (myocarditis, pericarditis, or coronary endarteritis) accounts for ~50% of deaths in EGPA; troponin T and NT-proBNP elevations demand urgent echocardiography and immediate immunosuppressive therapy.
  5. Role of Normal Myeloproliferative Biomarkers: Normal serum vitamin B12 (<1000 pg/mL) and normal serum tryptase (<11.5 ng/mL) help rule out myeloproliferative hypereosinophilic syndrome driven by FIP1L1-PDGFRA or KIT gene fusions.
  6. PR3-ANCA Positivity & GPA-Overlap Phenotype: While MPO-ANCA (p-ANCA) is classic for EGPA, 10–15% of patients test positive for PR3-ANCA (c-ANCA), which portends a vasculitic, GPA-like overlap phenotype with higher relapse rates.
  7. Radiographic Signature of Airway Eosinophilia: High-resolution chest CT demonstrating bronchial wall thickening, mucus plugging, tree-in-bud opacities, and fluctuating subsegmental atelectasis reflects intense eosinophilic endobronchial inflammation.
  8. Clonal Hematopoiesis of Indeterminate Potential (CHIP): In elderly patients with hypereosinophilia, somatic mutations in ASXL1 and TET2 at low variant allele fractions represent age-related CHIP rather than a primary eosinophilic myeloid neoplasm.
  9. IgE Spectrum in EGPA: Markedly elevated total serum IgE and elevated specific IgE against Aspergillus fumigatus can reflect generalized Th2 hyper-reactivity in EGPA and should not be mistaken for isolated Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis (ABPA).
  10. Targeted Anti-IL-5 Receptor Therapy (Benralizumab): Benralizumab (a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting the IL-5 receptor alpha chain) induces antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), producing rapid and complete depletion of eosinophils in blood and tissue.
  11. Steroid-Sparing Efficacy of Biologics: Targeted anti-IL-5 biologics (mepolizumab or benralizumab) permit significant reduction and ultimate discontinuation of systemic glucocorticoids while preventing disease flares.
  12. Systemic Glucocorticoid Induction: High-dose oral Prednisone (1 mg/kg/day with pulse methylprednisolone for organ-threatening disease) provides rapid anti-inflammatory debulking during acute EGPA induction.
  13. ANCA Seroconversion as a Biomarker: Serial monitoring of PR3-ANCA or MPO-ANCA titers provides a useful surrogate marker of disease activity and treatment response during immunosuppressive therapy.
  14. Strongyloides Screening Pre-Immunosuppression: In patients with severe hypereosinophilia receiving high-dose steroids, screening for Strongyloides stercoralis or empiric ivermectin prophylaxis is essential to prevent hyperinfection syndrome.
  15. Long-Term Relapse Surveillance: Because PR3-ANCA positive EGPA carries an elevated risk of late relapse, patients require long-term monitoring of AEC, ANCA titers, inflammatory markers (ESR/CRP), and cardiac function every 3–6 months.
