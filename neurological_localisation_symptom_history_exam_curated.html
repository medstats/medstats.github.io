Neurological Localisation
Symptom-Based History & Examination

Bedside teaching reference · History → Examination → Anatomical structure
Curated by Anupam Singh MD, Professor, Department of Medicine, Santosh Medical College
Core rule: Describe the symptom precisely, identify the second neurological abnormality, then ask: “What single anatomical lesion can explain all the findings?” The tables below are designed as a pre-case framework before working through localisation cases.

01

Diplopia

Localise: ocular surface → extraocular muscle/NMJ → CN III/IV/VI → MLF/PPRF → cavernous sinus/orbital apex → brainstem

How to think: Start with monocular vs binocular. Then define horizontal/vertical separation, gaze dependence, ptosis, pupil change, facial numbness, visual loss and long-tract signs.
Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit Structure / localisation
Diplopia persists when one eye is coveredCheck each eye separately; inspect ocular surface/refractionMonocular diplopia — eye/refractive media
Diplopia disappears when either eye is coveredConfirm ocular misalignment; test eye movements in all directionsBinocular diplopia — EOM/CN III, IV, VI/NMJ/brainstem
Horizontal separation of imagesTest abduction and adduction of each eyeCN VI/lateral rectus, medial rectus/CN III, MLF or horizontal gaze pathway
Vertical or oblique separationLook for hypertropia; test elevation/depression and head-tilt effectCN IV, CN III or central skew deviation
Worse looking toward one particular sideH-pattern; identify the weak muscle in its field of actionIndividual EOM / corresponding ocular motor nerve
Worse on reading or walking downstairsTest depression of the adducted eye; Bielschowsky head-tilt patternCN IV / superior oblique
Ptosis with diplopiaLook at eye position and pupilsCN III or NMJ; mild ptosis+miosis suggests sympathetic pathway
Ptosis/diplopia fluctuates and worsens later in daySustained upgaze; repeated saccades; look for fatigabilityNeuromuscular junction — myasthenia
Sudden headache with ptosis and diplopiaLook for complete III palsy and pupil enlargementCompressive CN III lesion; PCom aneurysm is an important cause
Diabetes/HTN with painless diplopia and ptosisIII palsy with preserved pupillary responseMicrovascular CN III palsy
Both eyes cannot look toward one sideTest conjugate horizontal gazeIpsilateral PPRF or abducens nucleus — pons
One eye fails to adduct; opposite eye has abducting nystagmusTest horizontal gaze and convergenceIpsilateral MLF — internuclear ophthalmoplegia
Diplopia + facial numbnessTest III/IV/VI and V1/V2 sensation/corneal reflexCavernous sinus / parasellar region
Diplopia + visual lossVisual acuity, colour vision, RAPD plus ocular movementsOrbital apex / optic nerve + ocular motor nerves
Diplopia + contralateral weaknessFull cranial nerve and UMN examinationBrainstem — midbrain or pons
Diplopia + vertigo/ataxiaNystagmus, skew, limb/gait coordinationBrainstem/cerebellum / vestibular pathways
Difficulty looking upward rather than isolated muscle weaknessUpgaze, convergence-retraction nystagmus, light-near dissociationDorsal rostral midbrain — Parinaud region

02

Aphasia

Localise the language network before naming the syndrome

How to think: First prove this is language dysfunction, not dysarthria, hearing loss, delirium or low consciousness. Test fluency, comprehension, repetition, naming, reading and writing.
Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit Structure / localisation
Knows what they want to say but cannot get words out; short effortful answersNonfluent speech; relatively preserved comprehension; impaired naming/repetitionDominant inferior frontal cortex — Broca/perisylvian frontal network
Speaks fluently but family says speech makes no senseFluent paraphasic speech; poor comprehension and repetitionDominant posterior superior temporal cortex — Wernicke network
Does the patient understand conversation?One-step then multistep verbal commandsPosterior temporal language comprehension network
Difficulty finding names of familiar objectsName pen/watch/body parts; describe object useDominant temporal–temporoparietal naming network
Fluent speech and comprehension good, but cannot repeatRepeat phrases of increasing complexityArcuate fasciculus/supramarginal region — conduction aphasia
Nonfluent speech but repetition surprisingly preservedFluency ↓, comprehension relatively good, repetition preservedDominant anterior watershed / medial frontal language network — transcortical motor
Fluent speech, poor comprehension, but repetition preservedConfirm preserved repetition despite poor comprehensionPosterior watershed / temporoparietal association cortex — transcortical sensory
Severe impairment of speech and comprehensionNonfluent + poor comprehension + poor repetitionLarge dominant perisylvian/MCA territory — global aphasia
Difficulty readingRead aloud; obey a written command; test reading comprehensionDominant occipitotemporal/angular language pathway
Cannot read but can still writeAsk spontaneous writing then ask patient to read itLeft occipital cortex + splenium — alexia without agraphia
Difficulty writingSpontaneous sentence, dictated sentence and copyingDominant parietal/angular or broader dominant language network
Calculation/right-left/finger identification difficultyAcalculia, agraphia, finger agnosia, right-left disorientationDominant angular gyrus — Gerstmann pattern
Understands command but cannot demonstrate learned actionPantomime brushing teeth, using a key or saluteDominant parietal–premotor praxis network
Aphasia with face/arm weakness greater than legRight lower facial and arm UMN signsDominant lateral hemisphere — usually left MCA cortex

03

Weakness — proximal vs distal

Use function, distribution, tone, reflexes and sensation to move from muscle to nerve to cord to brain

How to think: Do not stop at “power 4/5”. Ask what daily task fails and whether weakness is proximal or distal, symmetric or asymmetric, fatigable, painful, spastic or associated with sensory loss.
Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit Structure / localisation
Difficulty combing hair/reaching overheadShoulder abduction and external rotation; scapular movementProximal upper limb — muscle or C5–C6
Difficulty rising from chair without handsHip flexion/extension; sit-to-standProximal pelvic-girdle muscles / myopathy
Difficulty climbing stairs or getting up from squat/floorHip and knee proximal power; Gowers-type manoeuvreProximal lower limb / myopathy
Difficulty buttoning, turning key, opening bottleFinger abduction, opposition, grip, finger flexion/extensionDistal upper limb — C8–T1/root/plexus/median-ulnar-radial
Foot slaps or trips over toesHeel walking; dorsiflexion and toe extensionL4–L5/deep or common peroneal pathway
Cannot stand on toesSingle-leg heel raise; plantarflexion; Achilles reflexS1–S2 / tibial nerve
Symmetric proximal > distal weakness, no sensory lossProximal power, reflexes, muscle bulkMuscle / myopathy
Proximal weakness + ptosis/ophthalmoplegia + fatigabilitySustained upgaze; repetitive proximal movementsNeuromuscular junction — myasthenia

Face/arm weakness greater than legMotor homunculus distribution; language/neglectLateral motor cortex — MCA territory
Leg weakness greater than armLeg power, frontal signs, bladder symptomsMedial frontal motor cortex — ACA territory
Dense proportional face-arm-leg weakness without cortical signsNo aphasia/neglect/field defect; pure UMN patternInternal capsule
LMN weakness/wasting in arms + UMN signs in legsSegmental arm reflex loss + brisk legs/BabinskiCervical spinal cord
Bilateral UMN leg weakness, arms normalSensory level; brisk legs; BabinskiThoracic spinal cord
Asymmetric painful flaccid legs + absent reflexesSaddle sensation, root pattern, anal toneCauda equina
Dorsiflexion + eversion weak; inversion preservedTest inversion and hip abductionCommon peroneal nerve

04

Gait abnormality

Ask whether the gait problem is weakness, spasticity, ataxia, sensory loss, vestibular dysfunction, parkinsonism or frontal gait control

How to think: Describe the gait before naming it. Then test tone, reflexes, plantar responses, proprioception, Romberg, limb coordination, eye movements and distal power.

Falls consistently toward one sideLimb dysmetria; nystagmus; vestibular testingIpsilateral cerebellar/vestibular system

Vertigo and veering to one sideNystagmus; head impulse; gait veering/fallingVestibular apparatus/CN VIII/brainstem
Small steps, freezing, difficulty starting/turningStooped posture; reduced arm swing; shuffling; en-bloc turningBasal ganglia — parkinsonism
Feet seem 'stuck to floor'; cognition/bladder symptomsMagnetic gait; short steps; broad base; initiation difficultyFrontal-subcortical gait network
Legs buckle; difficulty rising from chairProximal power; Gowers-type manoeuvre; waddling gaitPelvic-girdle muscle

05

Numbness / sensory symptoms

Map the sensory pattern before interpreting modality

How to think: The distribution often localises first: hemisensory, crossed face-body, sensory level, dermatome, named nerve, stocking-glove or saddle.

Face, arm and leg numb on the same sideTest all modalities over face and bodyContralateral thalamus / sensory cortex

Numbness below a horizontal line on trunkMap pinprick upward to define levelSpinal cord
Band-like chest/abdominal sensationDermatomal level; UMN leg signsThoracic cord or root
Shooting pain from back into limbDermatomal sensory loss + root-specific weakness/reflex lossNerve root
Numbness in one named nerve territoryMap cutaneous distributionPeripheral nerve
Glove-and-stocking numbnessDistal pinprick/vibration loss + absent ankle jerksLength-dependent peripheral neuropathy
Worse balance in darknessVibration, joint position, RombergPosterior columns / large sensory fibres
Burns/injuries without noticing temperaturePain-temperature lost but vibration preservedCentral cord / syrinx
Saddle numbnessPerianal sensation, anal tone, sacral reflexesConus / cauda equina

Vertigo / dizziness

Separate peripheral vestibular disease from brainstem/cerebellar disease

How to think: Look for hearing symptoms, positional triggers, severe truncal ataxia and associated cranial-nerve or long-tract symptoms.
Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit Structure / localisation
True spinning sensationObserve spontaneous nystagmusVestibular system
Sudden vertigo lasting hours-days with nausea/vomitingHead impulse, nystagmus, skewPeripheral vestibular vs central brainstem/cerebellum
Vertigo + hearing loss/tinnitusBedside hearing; otologic examLabyrinth/CN VIII/AICA
Seconds-long vertigo triggered by turning in bedDix-HallpikePosterior semicircular canal — BPPV
Unable to stand or walk despite little subjective vertigoTruncal/gait ataxiaCerebellum
Vertigo + diplopia/dysarthria/dysphagiaCranial nerves, cerebellar and long-tract examBrainstem / posterior circulation
Vertigo + facial sensory loss + opposite body sensory lossCrossed sensory examinationLateral medulla/pons
Veers consistently to one sideTandem gait; limb coordinationIpsilateral vestibular/cerebellar pathway

07

Dysarthria, dysphagia & dysphonia

Differentiate language, articulation and voice

How to think: Aphasia = language. Dysarthria = articulation. Dysphonia = voice/larynx. Dysphagia may localise to corticobulbar, bulbar, NMJ or muscle.
Positive history findings / what to ask Positive examination findings / what to elicit Structure / localisation
Speech slurred but words/language appropriateArticulation testing; repetition of lingual/labial soundsMotor speech pathway — dysarthria
Nasal speech + nasal regurgitationPalatal elevationCN X / nucleus ambiguus
Hoarse voiceListen to phonation/cough; vocal cord examination if neededCN X / recurrent laryngeal
Chokes mainly on liquidsPalate, gag, coughOropharyngeal/bulbar pathway
Food remains in mouth / hard to manipulate bolusTongue movementsCN XII
Weak coughCough strengthCN X / bulbar muscles
Tongue wasting/fasciculationInspect tongue at rest and protrusionLMN XII / bulbar motor neuron
Brisk jaw jerk + slow stiff tongue without wastingJaw jerk, gag, limb UMN signsBilateral corticobulbar — pseudobulbar
Dysarthria + ipsilateral limb ataxiaFinger-nose, heel-shinCerebellum/brainstem
Dysarthria + clumsy hand, no aphasiaFine hand movements and pyramidal signsPons/internal capsule lacune

Use monocular vs binocular and field geometry to map the optic pathway

How to think: One eye = retina/optic nerve. Bitemporal = chiasm. Homonymous = retrochiasmal.

Bladder, bowel & sexual dysfunction

These symptoms can sharply separate cord, conus and cauda

How to think: Always ask in bilateral leg weakness: difficulty passing urine? altered perianal/genital sensation? bowel or sexual dysfunction?

First define the movement phenomenology; then localise the motor network

How to think: Rest tremor, intention tremor, ballistic movement, chorea, myoclonus and bradykinesia point to different networks.

Headache

Headache itself usually does not localise — associated neurological findings do

How to think: Define onset, speed to peak, position/Valsalva relation, fever, visual symptoms, focal deficits and cranial-nerve symptoms.

First decide whether this was true syncope, seizure or another transient neurologic event

How to think: True syncope reflects transient global cerebral hypoperfusion and usually does not localise to one lobe.
