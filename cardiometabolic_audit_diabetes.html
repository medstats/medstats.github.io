Cardiometabolic Treatment-Gap Clinical Audit in Diabetics

This dashboard presents a retrospective clinical audit of cardiometabolic treatment gaps among patients with diabetes and chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease and/or heart failure. 2615 Discharges were audited and 276 were found eligible .

The objective was to assess whether evidence-based therapies were documented at discharge in the patients for whom each therapy was clinically appropriate.

Headline finding

The largest treatment gaps were seen in GLP-1 RA use, finerenone eligibility, HFrEF MRA use, primary-prevention statin use and RAAS therapy. Antiplatelet therapy in CAD had the highest relative coverage among the major cardiovascular secondary-prevention classes.
  • RAAS in diabetic CKD: 9.6% coverage
  • SGLT2 inhibitor: 22.3% coverage
  • GLP-1 RA: 0.0% coverage
  • HFrEF beta-blocker: 27.6% coverage
  • HFrEF MRA: 17.4% coverage

Audit logic

Each therapy was evaluated only in the subgroup for whom it was clinically indicated. Where the registry suggested a plausible temporary reason for non-use, that case was separated from the probable-gap group.
  • Corrected urine microalbumin values were treated as ACR where the manual audit confirmed urine creatinine = 1 g/L.
  • RAAS decisions incorporated BP/HTN, eGFR, albuminuria, AKI, potassium and haemodynamic status.
  • MRA was separated into steroidal MRA for HFrEF and finerenone for diabetic kidney disease.
  • Antiplatelet non-use was allowed where OAC or major bleeding created a plausible reason.

How HOLD was interpreted

  • RAAS: unresolved AKI/renal deterioration, significant hyperkalaemia, or hypotension/shock.
  • SGLT2 inhibitor: active AKI, haemodynamic instability/shock, or other acute illness flags captured by the registry.
  • Antiplatelet: oral anticoagulation or major bleeding/haemorrhagic concern may make omission reasonable.
  • Beta-blocker: hypotension, marked bradycardia or conduction disease may justify withholding.
Important: HOLD is an audit classification, not proof that withholding was clinically correct. The final judgment still depends on full bedside context.
