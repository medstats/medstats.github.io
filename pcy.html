Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Pitfall of Pseudo-TTP (Schistocytosis without Hemolysis): Schistocytes on a peripheral blood smear are not pathognomonic for TTP; in bone marrow production failure, abnormal red cell dysmorphism and mechanical fragmentation occur without true microangiopathic intravascular destruction.
  2. Key Role of Normal ADAMTS13 Activity: An ADAMTS13 activity level ≥70% (76% in this case) definitively excludes Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) and mandates immediate discontinuation of therapeutic plasma exchange (PLEX).
  3. Discriminatory Power of Serum Haptoglobin: Normal or elevated serum haptoglobin (113 mg/dL) and normal bilirubin in a cytopenic patient argue strongly against intravascular microangiopathic hemolysis.
  4. Hypoproliferative Reticulocyte Index: A low reticulocyte index (<2.0%, calculated as 0.65% here) indicates bone marrow hypoproliferation and failure rather than peripheral destructive consumption.
  5. Dysplastic Smear Signatures: Morphologic features such as Pelger-Huët hypolobated/hypogranular neutrophils, giant platelets, and circulating immature blasts serve as key red flags for primary myelodysplasia.
  6. Classification of MDS with Excess Blasts 2 (MDS-EB2): MDS-EB2 is defined by 10% to 19% myeloblasts in the bone marrow or 5% to 19% blasts in the peripheral blood, representing a high-risk myeloid neoplasm prone to leukemic transformation.
  7. Extramedullary Myeloid Sarcoma (Chloroma): Myeloid neoplasms can present as extramedullary soft-tissue masses (e.g., bilateral adnexal masses mimicking Krukenberg tumors or ovarian carcinoma) with normal gynecologic tumor markers (CA-125, CEA).
  8. High-Risk Cytogenetic Profile: Complex cytogenetics involving Monosomy 5 (-5), 5q deletion (5q-), and Trisomy 8 (+8) portend a dismal prognosis and high IPSS-R/IPSS-M risk scores.
  9. Impact of TP53 Driver Mutations: Somatic mutations in TP53 (often biallelic) represent an adverse prognostic driver in MDS, conferring inherent resistance to conventional therapies and rapid leukemic progression.
  10. Role of DNMT3A Mutations: Co-occurring mutations in DNMT3A impair epigenetic DNA methylation and cooperate with TP53 to drive clonal expansion and aggressive myeloid disease.
  11. Mechanism of Hypomethylating Agents (Azacitidine): Azacitidine (an inhibitor of DNA methyltransferases) acts as disease-modifying hypomethylating therapy to induce hematologic responses and transfusion independence in high-risk MDS.
  12. Transient Response in TP53-Mutated MDS: Hypomethylating agent therapy in TP53-mutated MDS typically yields short-lived hematologic responses (median 6–9 months) before disease relapse occurs.
  13. Neutropenia & Opportunistic Infections: Severe neutropenia in MDS predisposes to mucosal fungal infections such as Candida esophagitis, which can manifest as dysphagia, odynophagia, and weight loss.
  14. Prompt Line Removal & Catheter Safety: Tunneled central lines placed for misdiagnosed TTP must be removed promptly upon excluding TTP to eliminate a major vector for bacteremia and neutropenic sepsis.
  15. Early Palliative Goals-of-Care Integration: Because TP53-mutated MDS-EB2 carries a dismal overall survival, early alignment of treatment goals, symptom management, and palliative hospice care is essential.
