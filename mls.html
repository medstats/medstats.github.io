Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Definition of MELAS Syndrome: MELAS (Mitochondrial Encephalomyopathy, Lactic Acidosis, and Stroke-like Episodes) is a maternally inherited multisystem mitochondrial disorder caused primarily by the m.3243A>G point mutation in the MT-TL1 gene.
  2. Stroke-like Episodes Crossing Vascular Boundaries: The radiologic hallmark of MELAS is cortical T2/FLAIR hyperintensity with diffusion restriction that ignores classical cerebral arterial territories, reflecting microvascular energy failure rather than thromboembolic occlusion.
  3. Classic Clinical Triad: The diagnostic clinical triad combines young-onset diabetes mellitus (MIDD phenotype), sensorineural hearing loss, and recurrent subacute stroke-like episodes with focal seizures.
  4. Persistent Lactic Acidosis Signature: Elevated resting serum or CSF lactic acid (3.0–5.0 mmol/L) without systemic anion gap acidosis is a key metabolic biomarker signaling mitochondrial respiratory chain dysfunction.
  5. Acute IV L-Arginine Therapy: Intravenous L-Arginine (0.5 g/kg bolus followed by continuous infusion) administered within 12–24 hours of stroke onset enhances endothelial nitric oxide synthesis and blunts microvascular ischemia.
  6. Lifelong Oral L-Arginine Prophylaxis: Daily oral L-Arginine (0.15–0.3 g/kg/day) and Taurine supplementation reduce the frequency and severity of recurrent stroke-like episodes.
  7. Strict Avoidance of Metformin: Metformin inhibits complex I of the mitochondrial electron transport chain and significantly increases the risk of severe, life-threatening lactic acidosis in MELAS patients.
  8. Contraindication of Valproic Acid: Valproic Acid is strictly contraindicated for seizure management in mitochondrial disorders due to severe hepatotoxicity, carnitine depletion, and POLG/mitochondrial inhibition.
  9. Preferred Antiepileptic Agents: Levetiracetam and Lacosamide are safe first-line antiseizure medications for controlling focal motor seizures and epilepsia partialis continua in MELAS.
  10. Metabolic Crisis Emergency Letter: Patients should carry a customized emergency protocol letter instructing urgent care teams to initiate IV 10% Dextrose and normal saline during febrile illnesses to prevent catabolic state triggering.
  11. Mitochondrial Cardiomyopathy Surveillance: All patients require baseline ECG, Holter monitoring, and echocardiography to screen for Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) syndrome, conduction blocks, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
  12. Diagnostic Heteroplasmy Variation: The proportion of mutant mtDNA (heteroplasmy) varies across tissues; urinary epithelial cells often yield higher m.3243A>G mutation burdens than peripheral blood leukocytes.
  13. MR Spectroscopy (MRS) Lactate Doublet: Proton MR Spectroscopy of stroke-like cortical lesions characteristically reveals an inverted lactate doublet at 1.33 ppm, confirming parenchymal lactic acidosis.
  14. Maternal Lineage Cascade Screening: Because mitochondrial DNA is strictly maternally inherited, confirmation of m.3243A>G mandates genetic counseling and screening for maternal relatives.
  15. Avoidance of Other Mitochondrial Toxins: Beyond Metformin and Valproate, patients should avoid Linezolid, Propofol infusions, and Aminoglycosides (which exacerbate sensorineural hearing loss).
