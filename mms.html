Resident:

Hello, Chief Resident here.

Key Findings: A 49-year-old severely immunosuppressed female (Adalimumab, Methotrexate, steroids) presents with chronic right elbow monoarthritis evolving into purulent sinus discharge, B-symptoms, and oliguria, signaling severe local infection and potential sepsis with acute kidney injury.

3. Opportunistic Fungal/Atypical Mycobacterial Infection (15%): Sporothrix, Histoplasma, or NTM secondary to potent immunosuppression.
4. Rheumatoid Nodule Breakdown with Secondary Infection (5%): Accelerated RA involvement complicated by skin breach.

Hello, Expert Clinician here.

Best unifying diagnosis: Right elbow tuberculous arthritis with contiguous osteomyelitis and draining sinus, facilitated by anti-TNF therapy, now possibly complicated by secondary pyogenic infection, sepsis, and acute kidney injury.

Top differentials
1. Pyogenic septic arthritis/osteomyelitis—especially S. aureus/MRSA: Purulence, fever, chills, and rapid deterioration fit; prior aspiration may have introduced infection. The preceding indolent monoarthritis and sinus/B symptoms favor TB or mixed infection.
2. Nontuberculous mycobacterial infection: Anti-TNF exposure and culture-negative chronic arthritis fit, but epidemiology and constitutional symptoms favor MTB.

Immediate move: Admit urgently; obtain blood cultures and operative synovial/bone tissue for bacterial, mycobacterial GeneXpert/culture, fungal studies, and histopathology, followed by orthopedic drainage/debridement. Hold adalimumab/methotrexate, assess septic AKI, and begin renal-adjusted empiric anti-MRSA plus gram-negative therapy after cultures. Obtain chest imaging and evaluate for disseminated/pulmonary TB.

Teaching pearl: Sinus swabs are inferior to deep tissue for diagnosing osteoarticular TB.

Top 15 Takeaways for Clinicians

  1. Anti-TNF Biologics and Disseminated Tuberculosis Risk: Biologic anti-TNF therapy (e.g., Adalimumab) severely impairs granuloma maintenance, predisposing patients to rapid hematogenous dissemination of M. tuberculosis to bone, joint, lungs, marrow, and pericardium.
  2. Pre-Biologic IGRA Limitations: A negative baseline pre-biologic IGRA (QuantiFERON-TB Gold) does not guarantee protection against newly acquired TB or delayed reactivation during ongoing anti-TNF immunosuppression.
  3. Frank Purulence Does Not Exclude Tuberculous Arthritis: Purulent sinus drainage and joint fluid can occur in tuberculous septic arthritis; routine bacterial culture negativity should immediately trigger mycobacterial NAATs (GeneXpert Ultra) and AFB cultures.
  4. Deep Operative Tissue Biopsy vs. Sinus Swabs: Operative synovial and bone biopsy yields significantly higher diagnostic sensitivity (up to 90%) than superficial sinus swabs or fluid aspiration.
  5. TB-Triggered Secondary Macrophage Activation Syndrome (MAS/HLH): Disseminated TB can trigger life-threatening secondary HLH/MAS in rheumatologic patients, presenting with hyperferritinemia (>4,500 ng/mL), cytopenias, and hypertriglyceridemia.
  6. TB Myopericarditis and Reversible Cardiogenic Shock: TB myopericarditis can present with acute cardiogenic shock and severe LV dysfunction (EF 10%); timely anti-TB therapy and corticosteroids can achieve complete functional recovery.
  7. Negative AFB Stains in Paucibacillary Extra-Pulmonary TB: Negative ZN AFB stains on bone marrow or synovial tissue sections do not rule out active disease; molecular NAAT (GeneXpert Ultra) and 16S sequencing provide rapid definitive confirmation.
  8. Role of Corticosteroids in TB Myopericarditis and MAS: High-dose IV corticosteroids (Dexamethasone/Prednisolone) are life-saving adjunctive therapies to suppress hyperinflammation, control myopericarditis edema, and prevent paradoxical IRIS.
  9. Permanent Biologic Withdrawal: Biologic anti-TNF agents must be permanently discontinued upon diagnosis of active disseminated TB, with subsequent RA control transitioned to non-biologic DMARDs.
  10. Extended Anti-Tubercular Therapy (ATT) Duration: Disseminated osteoarticular and myopericardial TB requires an extended 9 to 12-month course of ATT (2 months HRZE followed by 7–10 months HR).
  11. Repeat IGRA Nuances Under Immunosuppression: Low-borderline IGRA mitogen responses require cautious interpretation; repeat testing or T-Spot assays may unmask false-negative initial results.
  12. Monitoring ATT Hepatotoxicity and Drug Interactions: Prolonged ATT requires biweekly liver function monitoring and therapeutic drug monitoring in critically ill patients.
  13. Cardiovascular Surveillance in TB Myopericarditis: Serial echocardiography is mandatory to monitor LV ejection fraction recovery and screen for late constrictive pericarditis.
  14. Prevention of Paradoxical TB-IRIS: Abrupt cessation of anti-TNF therapy combined with rapid mycobacterial killing can precipitate TB-IRIS, which is effectively blunted by concomitant corticosteroid therapy.
  15. Public Health and Household Contact Screening: Every case of active disseminated TB mandates immediate public health reporting and screening of household contacts for latent or active infection.
