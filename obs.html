- Periumbilical abdominal pain for 3 months, which had become persistent and moderate in intensity over the last 15 days.

- Fever (reaching up to 102°F) for the last 10 days.

History details:

There was no history of hematuria, melena, chronic diarrhea, constipation, or jaundice.
  • Vomiting details: Insidious in onset, non-bilious, non-projectile, containing digested food particles and watery fluid. It was triggered by meal intake and was associated with a progressive decrease in appetite.
  • Fever details: Intermittent, insidious onset, initially low-grade, progressing to documented peaks of 102°F, relieved temporarily by antipyretics, with associated generalized bodyache. There was no history of night sweats, evening rise of temperature, cough, sputum production, dysuria, chills, rigors, or joint pain.
  • Co-morbidities: She had a 15-year history of Essential Hypertension and Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus under irregular medical treatment. She underwent a total abdominal hysterectomy 6 years ago (post-menopausal since then). No significant family history was reported.**

Best current unifying diagnosis: Ileocecal/intestinal tuberculosis causing an inflammatory stricture and partial small-bowel obstruction. This fits the endemic setting, prolonged midgut pain, meal-triggered vomiting, anorexia, fever, and recent worsening.
